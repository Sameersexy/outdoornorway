# Sharing and virality: what makes an ad worth sending

What it is: the research on why people forward content, what the platforms say they reward, and how to turn both into copy and into the group chat line. Read it at method step 8 when you write the group chat line, and whenever the user asks how to make an ad shareable.

Every example uses the invented company Brekke Ski Guides. Every example is a fictional brief. Prices, dates and group sizes are invented for teaching and carry no claim about any real operator. Brekke's invented ledger: day tour NOK 2,350, weekend course NOK 4,900, six day hut-to-hut trip NOK 18,500 (departures 14 and 21 Mar 2027, eight guests, two guides, five to seven hours a day on skins).

Evidence grades follow references/psychology.md: A peer reviewed and replicated, B large dataset or platform-official, C practitioner lore or theory with no direct ad evidence. Platform statements say "verified 2026-10-06" when the primary source was read that day, or carry [VERIFY: reason].

## 1. An honest frame: organic signals are not the ad auction

Two different systems get mixed up in marketing advice. Organic feeds rank posts by predicted behaviour. Paid ads run through an auction with its own delivery rules. The research below mostly describes people: what they forward and why. The platform signals tell you what each company says it rewards in organic feeds. Neither one is the ad auction.

Use organic signals as a guide to what people do with copy. Do not read them as a recipe for cheaper delivery. Meta's ad-level diagnostics (quality ranking, engagement rate ranking, conversion rate ranking) are the closest official ad signals, and the notes have them only from secondary sources [VERIFY: Meta's own help page returned no text; read it in a browser].

The skill never promises that an ad will go viral, get shared or convert. It states a test hypothesis and names what would count as a result.

## 2. What makes content worth sending

### 2.1 Arousal: awe, anger and anxiety travel, sadness does not

Berger and Milkman (2012) analysed all 6,956 New York Times articles that appeared on the home page between 30 August and 30 November 2008. Twenty percent reached the "most e-mailed" list. The figure below shows the change in the probability of making that list for a one standard deviation increase in each characteristic [https://jonahberger.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/ViralityB.pdf] (grade B).

| Characteristic | Change in probability of being most e-mailed |
|---|---|
| Anger | +34 percent |
| Awe | +30 percent |
| Practical value | +30 percent |
| Interest | +25 percent |
| Anxiety | +21 percent |
| Emotionality | +18 percent |
| Surprise | +14 percent |
| Positivity | +13 percent |
| Sadness | -16 percent |

The paper's reading is that high-arousal emotion, positive (awe) or negative (anger, anxiety), is more viral than low-arousal emotion such as sadness. The data are news articles shared by email in 2008. They are not ads and not X or Instagram. Treat the numbers as direction, never as a forecast.

What this means for a ski operator:

- Awe is honest and available: scale, light, silence, a descent. It needs a concrete scene. Stock scenery adjectives carry none.
- Anger is available only in a narrow, brand-safe form: a fair grievance about an industry habit that the operator can prove it avoids, such as a price buried on page three. Aim it at a habit, never at people.
- Anxiety maps to urgency, such as a count of places left. Use it only when the number is true and checkable. Fake scarcity is a trust and policy risk (ethics rules in references/psychology.md).
- Sadness and sentimental nostalgia lose on this evidence. Avoid them when the goal is sends and replies.

Give every ad a declared activation target: awe, a useful surprise or a fair grievance. Refuse low-arousal filler.

### 2.2 STEPPS

Jonah Berger's Contagious (2013) lists six principles: social currency, triggers, emotion, public, practical value and stories [summary: https://www.supersummary.com/contagious-jonah-berger/summary/] (grade C for use in ads, since the book is not an ad test). The practical value result is the one with a number, from the paper above.

| Principle | What it means | Ad-copy move | Example line (fictional brief) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social currency | Sharing makes the sender look knowledgeable or bold | Give the reader an insider fact they can repeat | "Why we ski north-facing slopes in March: the snow keeps its cold. [Operator confirms from the ledger.]" |
| Triggers | Frequent cues bring the idea to mind | Tie the offer to a daily or seasonal cue | "First snowfall of the season. Two March departures are on the page." |
| Emotion | High arousal is shared | One concrete awe scene | "Four hours up, then forty minutes down with our tracks only." |
| Public | Visible things get copied | Make the offer easy to see, name and tag | A place tag, a phrase that works without the caption |
| Practical value | People share useful things | Give the number, the list or the comparison | "What six days on skins cost, line by line." |
| Stories | People carry ideas inside stories | Put the offer inside a plot with a turn | "At 09:10 we reached the ridge. Nobody spoke for a minute." |

The New York Times Customer Insight Group's 2011 "Psychology of Sharing" study is reported to say that 94 percent of respondents consider how useful the information will be to the recipient before sharing [secondary: https://contently.com/2012/02/24/psychology-of-sharing/] [VERIFY: the primary report was not opened]. The direction fits the practical value result.

In the brief, ask six yes or no questions, one per principle. In the receipt, name the one or two principles a variant relies on.

### 2.3 Information gap, used honestly

Loewenstein (1994) described curiosity as the felt gap between what you know and what you want to know [secondary: https://www.ignorancegraph.com/information-gaps/information-gap-theory/]. A safe working version: curiosity needs some prior knowledge and a visible missing piece [VERIFY: the primary text was an unreadable scan, so do not quote any relationship between gap size and curiosity].

A headline can open a gap. The landing page must close it with a real, checkable payoff in the first screen. Meta's Advertising Standards state ads must not contain shocking or sensational content [https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/], and secondary guides report reduced distribution for clickbait [https://savemyleads.com/blog/other/meta-ads-guidelines] [VERIFY: the exact engagement bait wording on Meta's own page before it goes into the skill].

Example (fictional brief): "The one gully we only ski when the wind drops. How we decide, on one page." The page must show the decision rule. If it shows a booking form, the ad is bait.

### 2.4 Specificity and concreteness

Two sources with real data (grade B):

- Hansen and Wänke (2010): the same content is judged more likely true when written in concrete language than in abstract language, across four experiments [https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167210386238].
- Hu, Pilgrim, Zhao and Hills (2025): concrete language predicted sharing in 15.5 million Twitter posts (February 2020 to January 2021, 35 topics) and 50,517 Reddit posts. In an experiment with 167 participants over 55 pairs of manipulated posts people chose the concrete version more often (t(166) = 15.36, p < .001). Each standardised unit of concreteness went with about 216 more Reddit upvotes. The authors note that concreteness was scored word by word, that platform algorithms and follower counts were not modelled, and that concreteness and specificity correlate only weakly (r = .255) [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13062457/].

Swap category words for sensory, countable things. The stock brochure promise becomes "four hours of skinning, then a descent with nobody else on it". Numbers help because a reader can check them. Require at least one number or named place in every variant, and a ledger row for each.

### 2.5 Identity and social currency

Berger and Heath (2007) found people diverge from the majority in domains that signal identity, such as music and hairstyles, more than in domains that do not, such as backpacks [Journal of Consumer Research 34(2): https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/34/2/121/1793110] (grade B). For an identity-relevant product the copy should let the buyer say who they are without being told who they are.

Two tests, both labelled heuristics because no study under these names was found:

- Identity line test: would a customer repeat this sentence about themselves? Example (fictional brief): "For people who ski for the walk up."
- Quote-post test, for X: would a reader quote-post this line to sound sharper? A line that passes states something the reader wishes they had said first.

Name an identity by behaviour. A personal attribute is off limits on Meta (policy detail in references/policy.md).

### 2.6 Moral emotion does not travel into ads

Brady and colleagues (2017, PNAS) reported that each additional moral-emotional word raised diffusion by about 20 percent in polarised political discussions [https://csmapnyu.org/research/academic-research/emotion-shapes-the-diffusion-of-moralized-content-in-social-networks] [VERIFY: the 20 percent figure and the replication against the PNAS abstract]. The data are political tweets. In ads, moral outrage invites policy flags, comment wars and brand damage. Keep it out of the default voice.

## 3. Private sharing and the group chat line

Most sharing a brand cannot see happens in private: direct messages and group chats. Alexis Madrigal coined "dark social" in The Atlantic in 2012. A secondary summary reports that one day's Chartbeat data put 56 percent of social traffic to The Atlantic in dark social channels against 21.6 percent from Facebook [https://www.cmoalliance.com/dark-social-tracking-and-leveraging-private-sharing/] [VERIFY: the primary Atlantic article was not opened]. Newer headline figures (69 percent of sharing in 2014, 84 percent recently) come from vendor blogs with unclear methods and are not used here [VERIFY before any use].

The platforms now count private sends. The X ranking model predicts "share via DM" and "share via copy link" as separate positive actions (section 4). Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, reportedly named "sends per reach" among the top ranking signals in January 2025, through secondary sources only [VERIFY]. A claim that sends are worth three to five times a like for reach to non-followers circulates widely, and the one article the notes checked attributes it to unnamed third-party reports, not to Instagram [https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance] [VERIFY: treat as unconfirmed].

### 3.1 The design rule

An ad is sendable when it works as one message in a chat with no context. A friend receives the image or the line plus "look at this" or "we should do this in March". The copy has to survive being forwarded: complete, concrete and plainly about a place, a date and a price.

### 3.2 The group chat line

Every set of variants ends with one group chat line: a sentence written to be pasted to a friend, a partner or a group. The ad itself never asks anyone to share (section 6). The line is for the champion, the person inside the group who carries the idea to the others (see references/buyer-journey-and-anxieties.md).

Rules for the line:

1. One to three sentences, in the voice of a friend, not a brand. A practitioner target is under about 200 characters, because long pastes get skimmed [heuristic, no source].
2. It holds what, when, how much (the total) and one line on level or safety, the facts a second reader asks for first.
3. Every fact matches a ledger row and the landing page. The line adds no claim the ads do not make.
4. It ends with a human move: a question or a plan for two ("Free the 14th?").
5. No link, hashtag, emoji or brand slogan. No "check this out".
6. It reads as something a person could have typed without having seen the ad.

Weak and strong, with the reason (fictional brief). The weak line contains patterns the skill bans, so it sits in a lint ignore block.

<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-start -->

Weak: "Check out this amazing ski tour deal! Tag a friend who needs a holiday!"

<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-end -->

Why it fails: it has no date, no price and no place, so the second reader learns nothing. It asks for a tag, which is engagement bait. It sounds like an ad.

Strong: "Found a six-day ski tour in the Sunnmøre Alps, 14 or 21 Mar. Huts, two guides, eight people, NOK 18,500 all told. Level is red runs with control. Free the 14th?"

Why it works: the first two sentences answer what, when and how much, the three questions a second reader asks first. The level line answers "can I do it". The last line is a plan for two people. It reads like a person wrote it. The count is 160 characters.

Check the facts and the length in code, never by eye. If a ledger fact is missing (no level, no price), the skill writes a shorter line and says what is missing.

### 3.3 The forward test

Paste the ad text and the link preview into a blank chat. If a reader cannot tell what it is, when it is and what it costs without the picture, rewrite. Run this on every variant before the final output.

## 4. Platform signals

Platform behaviour changes faster than craft. Every statement here was verified 2026-10-06 against the source named, or carries [VERIFY: reason]. Recheck anything older than 90 days.

### 4.1 X organic For You ranking

Primary source, read on 2026-10-06: the xai-org/x-algorithm repository, first published 2026-01-20 with a major update on 2026-05-15 [https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm; the update date is from a secondary source, https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/2594/x-algorithm-open-source-may-2026] (grade B, platform-official).

What the README states:

- A learned ranking model predicts the probability of these actions. Engagement: favorite, reply, repost, quote, share, share via DM, share via copy link. Clicks: post, profile, link, photo expand, video open, quoted post. Attention: video quality view, dwell, dwell time, click dwell time, video continuation seconds, profile visit seconds. Author: follow. Negative: not interested, mute, block, report, not dwelled.
- The score is a weighted sum of weight times predicted probability. The weights sit in home-mixer/params/param.rs, which the notes did not read, so no weight is quoted [VERIFY: read param.rs before quoting any weight]. Older third-party claims about numeric weights, such as "a reply is worth 27 likes", come from the 2023 release and do not describe the 2026 system.
- After scoring: author diversity (repeat posts from one author decay), an out-of-network discount and a new-author boost for low-impression authors.

Links: the README lists a link click as a positive action and the notes found no link penalty in it. In July 2026 Elon Musk replied "We haven't for over a year" to a question about penalising links, and X product head Nikita Bier said links no longer need to go in replies [https://adtechradar.com/2026/07/28/x-throttling-posts-with-links/]. That article notes it gives no code evidence [VERIFY: read it as "no known explicit penalty"; reach can still fall if fewer people click through].

Scope: this is the organic ranker. Paid X ads run through the ads stack with their own auction [VERIFY with references/x.md].

### 4.2 Instagram organic

Adam Mosseri reportedly named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top signals in January 2025, with likes slightly more important for connected reach and sends slightly more for unconnected reach. The notes found this through secondary sources only [https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance, https://gyre.pro/blog/instagram-algorithm-updates-and-tips-to-boost-your-visibility] [VERIFY: locate Mosseri's original post before quoting]. Feed, Reels, Stories and Explore reportedly use separate ranking systems.

### 4.3 Meta ads

- Ad-level diagnostics show quality, engagement rate and conversion rate rankings, rated above average, average or below average, after about 500 impressions [secondary: https://adriselab.com/learn/ad-relevance-diagnostics] [VERIFY: Meta's page returned no body text].
- Andromeda is the retrieval stage of Meta's ad system, described in Meta's engineering blog of 2 December 2024 [https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/] (grade B). The popular rules about a "Creative Similarity Score" above 60 percent are agency lore, traced to a vendor guide and not to Meta [VERIFY: no Meta source]. What Meta itself advised in December 2025, as reported, is to supply a broad portfolio of creative [https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182/].
- The safe takeaway for the variants command: write conceptually different ads and never paraphrases. Two ads that differ only in the headline are one idea.

### 4.4 What the signals mean for copy

| Signal the platform names | Copy behaviour that earns it |
|---|---|
| Reply, quote | A line with a clear stance, or an open question the reader can answer |
| Share via DM, copy link, sends per reach | A complete, useful, concrete message that makes sense forwarded |
| Dwell, watch time | A first line that earns the second line, with no padding |
| Likes per reach | Immediate resonance with the identity of the audience |
| Negative actions (mute, block, report, not interested) | Avoid bait, vague promises, hostile tone and repetition |
| Author diversity decay (X) | Do not post five near-identical posts in a row |

## 6. What not to do

- No engagement bait. The ad never asks for likes, tags or shares. Meta reduces distribution for engagement bait according to secondary summaries [https://savemyleads.com/blog/other/meta-ads-guidelines] [VERIFY: Meta's own wording]. The group chat line is for the user's own use and appears in the output as a separate item.
- No invented proof to make a line more shareable.
- No moral outrage and no contrarian jab at a group of people. A contrarian line works when it is a claim about the craft or the category that the reader can test, and the operator can prove its own side. There is no peer reviewed source for this, so it is judgement and not science.
- No claim that the ad will go viral, beat a detector or lift conversions.
- No format trick (all caps, odd symbols, bait punctuation) to force attention. The interrupt has to come from content: a specific number, a plain sentence in a feed of hype. "Pattern interrupt" is practitioner language with no academic source under that name, and the nearest research idea is the isolation (von Restorff) effect [VERIFY: primary not looked up].
- No volume play. Five near-identical posts in a row lose reach on X (author diversity decay) and count as one idea on Meta.

## 7. How the skill uses this file

1. In the brief, ask the six STEPPS questions and the two heuristic tests (identity line, quote-post).
2. Give every ad an activation target and name it in the receipt.
3. Require one number or named place per variant, mapped to the ledger.
4. Write the group chat line last, from the final ledger facts, and run the forward test on it.
5. Label every platform statement with its date. Update this file when a platform changes.
6. Report a test hypothesis with each variant. For sharing, a candidate metric is shares per impression on the ad, with the caveat that availability varies by objective and placement [VERIFY in Ads Manager].

Example hypothesis (fictional brief): "If the concrete scene earns more forwards than the price-led variant, its share rate per impression will be higher over the same budget. If it does not, concreteness is not the lever for this audience."
