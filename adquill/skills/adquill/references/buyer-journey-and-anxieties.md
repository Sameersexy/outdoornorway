# Buyer path and anxieties for high-ticket experiences

What it is: how people decide to buy a premium guided trip, the job an ad has at each stage, the anxiety list with copy answers that stay inside Meta's personal attributes rule, the group decision problem, price psychology and a short consumer law pointer. Read it at method step 3 when you diagnose the main anxiety, and whenever the offer costs more than a few thousand NOK or more than one person has to say yes.

Every example uses the invented company Brekke Ski Guides. Every example is a fictional brief. Prices, dates, group sizes, policies and inclusions are invented for teaching and carry no claim about any real operator. Brekke's invented ledger: day tour NOK 2,350, weekend course NOK 4,900, six day hut-to-hut trip NOK 18,500 (departures 14 and 21 Mar 2027, eight guests, two guides, five to seven hours a day on skins), deposit NOK 3,000, full refund when the guest cancels 60 days or more before departure.

Evidence grades follow references/psychology.md: A peer reviewed and replicated, B large dataset or platform-official, C practitioner lore or a model with little formal test. Anything unverified carries [VERIFY: reason].

## 1. Ten rules in one place

1. The ad does not sell the trip. It starts or restarts a decision that runs for weeks and passes through other people.
2. Social proof was the largest or second-largest lever in 28 of 31 categories in Google's test. Specific, real reviews beat adjectives.
3. Scarcity was the weakest of six levers in the same test and can feel restrictive to someone still exploring. Use it only when true, and only late.
4. Experiences pay back in anticipation. The ad can sell the weeks of looking forward, not only the days on snow.
5. For a purchase this size, ability (money, time off, brain effort, social friction) is the binding constraint. Cut effort first and add motivation second.
6. The person who clicks is rarely the only person who must say yes. Write so the ad survives being pasted into a group chat.
7. Name the anxiety through the trip, never through the viewer. "Eight guests, two guides" passes Meta policy. A question about the viewer's body does not.
8. Show the total price, what it covers and what it costs to cancel. Per-day framing supplements the total and never replaces it.
9. Fake urgency is illegal in Norway and the EU, and Meta can reject it. It is also the weakest lever. It has no upside.
10. Several famous findings are shaky (loss aversion size, the decoy effect in real settings, some work by Dan Ariely). Prefer principles with independent support.

## 2. What the research says about the path to purchase

### 2.1 Google's messy middle (grade B)

Google and The Behavioural Architects observed 310 shopping paths in 31 categories in the UK, then ran a simulation with 31,000 online shoppers (July 2020). The model: a trigger starts the search, and the buyer loops between exploring (widening the options) and evaluating (narrowing them) until purchase. The report found no typical path, only a confusing web of touchpoints. [https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/9998/Decoding_Decisions_The_Messy_Middle_of_Purchase_Behavior.pdf]

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Social proof largest or second-largest effect | 28 of 31 categories | Google report, link above |
| Power of free largest or second-largest | 18 of 31 categories | Same |
| Scarcity | Most often the least effective of the six biases tested | Same |
| Package holiday: shoppers switching to their second favourite brand with no bias applied | 34 percent | Same |
| Package holiday: all six biases applied to the second favourite | 88 percent drawn away from the favourite | Same |

Limits: UK shoppers in 2020, simulated purchases of fictional brands, no ski trips, no purchase near NOK 18,500. The package holiday figures support one message: loyalty is soft in travel, so a better evidenced offer can win.

### 2.3 What ski buyers research (closest data found, grade C)

An Iglu survey of UK skiers (sample size and method not stated, [VERIFY: sample size and method]) reported snow reliability as the top resort factor at 78 percent, value for money at 56 percent, "been before" as the most trusted source at 55 percent, word of mouth at 51 percent and reviews at 42 percent [https://www.snowsportsnews.com/newsrepository/2026/june/in-iglu-s-biggest-ever-survey-ai-use-for-ski-trip-research-nearly-quadruples-in-a-year-but-snow-certainty-and-trusted-advice-still-decide-where-britain-skis/]. Intermediates were 46 percent of that market. These are UK mass-market package buyers, so they do not describe guided off-piste clients. They hint at the questions to answer: snow, level, value and a voice the buyer trusts.

What social advertising does in this path: it feeds inspiration, it re-enters during evaluation as retargeting and proof, and it rarely closes alone. Expect assisted conversions and say so in the brief. X is a conversation platform, so its role is voice, authority and replies rather than scenic reels.

## 3. The four stages and the job of the ad at each

The stages come from Google's loop, Moesta's forces, the Expedia and Iglu path data and Fogg's model. The buyer loops between stages 2 and 3 several times, and a person at stage 4 can slide back to stage 2 when a rival's review appears. Keep proof flowing during the loop. Stage lengths are indicative.

| Stage | What is happening | The buyer's question | Dominant forces | Job of the ad | Levers (rows in references/psychology.md) | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Trigger | First snow, a friend's photo, a bad week at work, a birthday, last season's regret | "Could I do something like that?" | Push, pull | Interrupt with one vivid, specific scene and a hint at who it is for. Start the anticipation. No price pressure. | 21, 24, 25, 7, 22 | Meta Reels and feed video. X short text with one strong image. |
| 2. Consideration | Comparing resorts, operators, reviews, flights. The shortlist forms over weeks. | "Is this one of the right ones? Why here, why these people?" | Pull against habit | Earn shortlist status. Show the case for the place, the guide's credibility, the shape of the trip and real proof. | 1, 2, 4, 5, 27, 6 | Carousel, short guide video, review cards, retargeting. X threads in the guide's voice. |
| 3. Doubt | The group chat, the partner, the spreadsheet, the worry | "Can I do it? Is it safe? Is it worth it? What if it goes wrong? Will my group say yes?" | Anxiety | Answer the one objection the buyer cannot say out loud. Arm the champion for the second reader. Lower the ability barriers. | 16, 15, 33, 8, 39, 3 | Retargeting to site visitors, FAQ carousels, click-to-chat, a forward-ready summary. |
| 4. Decision | A date is picked, a deposit paid, or the idea is dropped | "What is the next small step? What happens after I pay?" | Ability, prompts | Make the next step tiny and clear: dates, total price, deposit, what happens next. Real urgency only when a real deadline exists. | 38, 12, 11, 18, 20, 9 | Retargeting with dates and price. X reply to enquiries. |
| 5. After booking | Waiting for departure, sharing with friends, the trip, the memory | "Did I make the right call?" | Habit, relatedness | Mostly outside paid ads. The same team can use email and WhatsApp to keep the countdown warm and to collect reviews with consent, which feed stages 2 and 3. | 30, 35, 36, 37 | Owned channels. |

Rules for the skill:

- Ask first which stage this audience is in. One stage per ad. A trigger ad that quotes a per-day price is a mismatch, and so is a decision ad that opens with a mountain poem.
- Use the stage to choose the lever, then name the lever in the receipt.
- For a campaign, build a family and not one ad that tries every job. A workable family per trip: two or three scenes for stage 1, two proof ads for stage 2, two objection or champion ads for stage 3 and one facilitator ad for stage 4 [practitioner pattern, grade C]. The variants command produces conceptually different angles of one offer. A family spans stages, and each ad in it still carries one idea.

Example, one trip across stages (fictional brief):

| Stage | Line | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Seven a.m. on the ridge. The only sound is your skins." | A scene that holds the reader's picture before any price appears. |
| 2 | "Two guides, eight guests, five to seven hours a day on skins. Huts, not hotels." | Shape of the trip, checkable facts. |
| 3 | "Cancel 60 days or more before and we refund you in full. If the forecast closes a route, the guide picks another." | One risk answered with the exact rule. |
| 4 | "14 or 21 Mar. NOK 18,500 all told. Hold a place with NOK 3,000. Booking for 14 Mar closes 1 Feb." | Dates, total, deposit and a real deadline. |

## 4. The four forces and the jobs behind a trip

Moesta's model has four forces. Push is frustration with the present. Pull is the attraction of the new. Anxiety is fear about the new. Habit is comfort with the old. Change happens when push plus pull beat anxiety plus habit [https://jobstobedone.org/radio/unpacking-the-progress-making-forces-diagram/]. It is a practitioner model built from interviews, not a lab result (grade C), and it makes a good copy tool.

| Force | Example | Where it shows up in copy |
|---|---|---|
| Push | Same resort every year. A grey winter at the desk. Friends drifting apart. "I keep saying I will go." | The hook and the trigger moment |
| Pull | A line seen in a film. A week with friends. A new level. | The core promise and the imagery |
| Anxiety | Am I good enough? Is it safe? Is the money wasted? Will there be snow? | Mid-ad proof, FAQ, risk reversal |
| Habit | The usual Alps package. "We always go to the same valley." | A contrast line that respects the habit |

The job is rarely "ski". It is closer to "be somebody who does this with people I like", "get back the feeling of being good at something" or "have a story to tell". Each is a job someone can hire a trip for. Write toward the job.

An ad should address at least one push or pull and one anxiety or habit force. When the operator has no guest interviews, the skill flags the guess. Five questions to ask recent guests, adapted from the switch-interview idea:

1. When did you first think about a trip like this, and what was going on that week?
2. What else did you consider, including doing nothing?
3. What nearly stopped you?
4. Who did you talk to, and what did they ask?
5. What made you pay the deposit on that day and not a week later?

## 5. Ability: what stands between interest and a booking

Fogg's model says behaviour happens when motivation, ability and a prompt meet at the same moment [https://www.behaviormodel.org/]. It is a design checklist with little formal test (grade C). For a trip near NOK 18,500, ability is usually the binding constraint. The factor list below is Fogg's as the notes record it [VERIFY: the six factors come from Fogg's writing and the home page did not list them].

| Ability factor | What it looks like for a ski trip | Ad or landing page fix |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Total cost, flights on top, gear | Show the total, what is included, what is not, and a deposit step |
| Time | Days off work, school holidays | Show dates early. Offer a short first step such as the day tour at NOK 2,350 |
| Physical effort | Fitness, long days | State the day profile, hours on skins and the pace rule |
| Brain cycles | Comparing resorts, flights, operators | One page, one clear package, one next step |
| Social deviance | "Is this a silly spend?" "Will my group come?" | Proof from people like them and a shareable summary |
| Non-routine | Booking abroad, a new operator, new terrain | Plain booking steps, clear policies, a human to message |

When motivation is already high, because the person sits in a ski group chat in October, the ad's best job is a facilitator: fewer clicks and clearer answers. When motivation is low, as with a cold audience, the ad is a spark: a scene. Ask "is this audience motivation-limited or ability-limited?" before writing, and check that the landing page removes the barrier the ad names.

## 6. The anxiety list and how copy answers each

The anxiety force is what ads skip most often. The evidence base is thin. One peer reviewed study found lower fear and higher self-efficacy went with better beginner performance in ski learning [https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6162698/], but most of what is known about travel anxieties comes from ski school and forum writing (grade C). Treat the list as hypotheses to test against the operator's own enquiries and reviews.

### 6.1 The rule

Answer the anxiety with a fact about the trip, written as a statement about the offering ("Eight guests, two guides") or as a question about the trip ("How does the guide handle mixed levels?"). Never write a question that opens with "Are you" or "Do you" followed by a body, health, age, relationship or money word.

Meta's Ad Standards say ads must not "assert or imply personal attributes". The list includes race, ethnicity, religion, age, sexual orientation or practices, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health, medical conditions, vulnerable financial status, voting status, trade union membership and criminal record. "You" language is allowed as long as it does not reference a personal attribute. The policy's own pair: a line offering depression counselling is allowed, and a line asking whether depression is getting someone down is not [https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/, read 2026-10-06 through a summarising fetch, confirm in the live policy]. Relationship status is a sensitive area to avoid as well [VERIFY against the current Meta policy list].

### 6.2 The list

All example lines: fictional brief, Brekke Ski Guides. Facts that need a ledger row (certifications, counts, policies) are marked in brackets.

| Anxiety | What the buyer is really asking | How copy answers (about the trip) | Example line (fictional brief) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Am I fit enough? | "Will I be the problem?" | State the day profile: hours on skins, pace, rest stops, route options. | "Five to seven hours a day on skins, with a stop every hour. The guide sets the pace." |
| Am I good enough? | "Will I embarrass myself?" | Name level bands and say what each can do. | "Level 3: red runs with control in variable snow. We teach the rest." |
| Will I be the slowest? | Fear of holding the group back | Say how the pace is set and who stays at the back. | "Two guides, eight guests. The pace follows the group, and one guide stays at the back." |
| Will there be snow? | Snow was the top factor in the Iglu survey (78 percent, grade C) | Say the plan when conditions are poor and the refund rule. Be honest about risk. | "If the forecast closes a route, the guide picks another. If the whole week is unsafe, [the refund rule from the ledger]." |
| Is it safe? | Avalanche, injury, weather | Certifications, group size, kit, decision rules. Authority helps most where the buyer lacks knowledge. | "Two guides, a transceiver check every morning and a turn-round time agreed the night before. Guides: [certifying body]." |
| Is it worth the money? | A large spend | Show the total and the stack. Add a sourced comparison if one exists. | "NOK 18,500: two guides, five nights in huts, breakfast and dinner. About NOK 3,083 a day." |
| Solo travel | "Will I be alone for a week?" | Describe the group, never the traveller. Real numbers only. | "Come with friends or on your own. Eight guests, two guides, one table at night." |
| Cold | "Will I be miserable?" | Gear list, warm stops, typical temperatures with a source. | "Gear list the day you book. Hot lunch at the hut on days two to five." |
| Gear | "Do I need to buy everything?" | What is lent, what to bring, what hire costs. | "We lend the avalanche kit. You bring boots and clothing. Tour skis for hire at NOK 650 a day." |
| Logistics | "How do I get there?" | Where the trip starts, how long from the nearest city, what the operator arranges. | "We meet in Ålesund on Sunday at 08:30 and drive you to the trailhead." |
| What if I cannot go? | Cancellation and insurance | The plain terms. Never imply cover you do not provide. | "Cancel 60 days or more before and we refund you in full. Travel insurance is not included." |
| Group fit | "Will I like the other guests?" | Group size and guest mix by behaviour. | "Most of us like an 08:30 start and a long lunch." |

Add a "never promise" line to the anxiety about snow: no "guaranteed powder", no "never cold", no "anyone can do this". Each is a misleading-claim risk.

## 7. Group decision-making and the champion

### 7.1 What the research says

Skiing is a group activity. In the Iglu survey 37 percent travelled with family, 31 percent as a couple and 28 percent with friends (grade C, link in section 2.3). Tourism research on couples reports a trend toward joint decisions, with women often dominant in the early stages [Couple dynamic in household tourism decision making: women as gatekeepers?, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254583454_Couple_Dynamic_in_Household_Tourism_Decision_Making_Women_as_the_Gatekeepers, grade C, abstract level] [VERIFY: the stage pattern]. Practitioner writing on ski groups says each group needs one organiser who keeps the plan moving [https://www.snowcompare.com/blog/2024/09/ski-trip, grade C]. No peer reviewed ski-specific study of the second reader was found [VERIFY: treat as a hypothesis].

Private sharing matters. A GWI and We Are Social study reported 63 percent of UK and US internet users share content through private messaging apps, ahead of open social media at 54 percent [https://www.thedrum.com/news/63-people-prefer-share-content-dark-social-channels, grade C, dated] [VERIFY: year and method].

### 7.2 The second reader

The person who clicks the ad is the champion. The people who must say yes are the second readers: the partner, the friend who is the weakest skier, the friend who holds the budget. The second reader sees the ad cold, in a chat thread, with the champion's one-line comment above it. They ask, in this order:

1. What is this? (one sentence)
2. When and how long? (dates)
3. How much, for what? (total price and inclusions)
4. Can I do it? (level, fitness, age mix of the group)
5. Is it safe and reputable?
6. What if I cannot go? (cancellation)
7. Who else is going?

The champion needs a link preview that works without context (title, image, price visible), a short block they can paste (trip name, dates, price, one line on level, one on safety), a reason that sounds like an idea the second reader would have, and a way to take a small step without the whole group.

### 7.3 How an ad arms the champion

| Tactic | How | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Forward test | Paste the ad text and the preview into a blank chat. Do questions 1 to 3 survive without the picture? | Pass, or rewrite |
| Champion line | A copy-ready sentence: trip, dates, total price, guided, one line on level. | Facts match the landing page |
| Cost per person | Show the per-person total and the room-sharing assumption. | Matches the booking terms |
| Group-ready proof | Review quotes from groups ("six of us, mixed levels") as well as solo skiers. | Real, with consent |
| A line for the sceptic | One FAQ line aimed at the doubter: "Mixed levels? The guide splits the day." | Only if the operator really does |
| Click-to-chat | A WhatsApp or Messenger reply path for "I have a question for the group". | Click-to-WhatsApp availability and Norwegian rules [VERIFY in Ads Manager]. The reply time promise must be realistic. |
| Date poll | A landing page tool where a group votes on two or three dates. | A product decision for the operator |

The champion line is separate from the ad copy. The skill writes it as the "group chat line" at the end of every set, and references/sharing-and-virality.md says how to write it. It never invents group size or date facts. Those come from the brief.

Example champion line (fictional brief): "Six-day ski tour in the Sunnmøre Alps, 14 or 21 Mar 2027. NOK 18,500 per person, two guides, eight guests, huts and two meals a day included. Level: red runs with control. Refund in full up to 60 days before. Hold a place with NOK 3,000."

## 8. Price psychology for premium experiences

All numbers in the examples are the invented Brekke ledger. Calculations are done in code before they are printed: 18,500 divided by 6 is 3,083.33, so "about NOK 3,083 a day" is a true statement. Offer templates live in references/offer-and-price.md. This section gives the reasons.

| Question | Evidence and grade | Rule for copy |
|---|---|---|
| Per-day or total? | Gourville (1998) showed per-day framing raises compliance because it moves the comparison to small recurring expenses [https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/24/4/395/1797969] (B). It needs a plausible small-expense comparison. Package travel is marketed at a total price [https://lovdata.no/artikkel/pakkereiser/5166] [VERIFY with counsel]. | Total first, per-day second, same line. Never per-day alone. |
| Which anchor? | The first number a reader sees becomes the reference (Tversky and Kahneman, 1974: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124) (B). Experts shift less [https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7434854/]. | Use a real, dated, like-for-like reference only. Guided days and unguided days are different products. |
| Bundle or itemise? | Thaler (1985): people keep separate mental budgets and a trip sits in the treat account [https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:4:y:1985:i:3:p:199-214] (B). Buyers dislike discovering add-ons. | One total, then a concrete stack: guides, huts, meals. List what is not included. |
| Hide fees until checkout? | On StubHub, hiding mandatory fees until checkout raised revenue by about 21 percent and made buyers 14.1 percent more likely to complete [https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25186/w25186.pdf] (B). | The temptation is real and regulators target it. Show inclusions and exclusions up front. |
| Deposit? | Prelec and Loewenstein (1998) predict people prefer to prepay for consumption so it feels free [https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.17.1.4] (C for the copy move). A small deposit also lowers the entry barrier. | Say what the deposit is, when the balance is due and what happens if plans change. |
| Cancellation? | The anxiety force is mostly risk. Evidence is vendor data only: an Expedia partner case study reported a flexible policy lifted bookings by 26 percent and revenue by 53 percent per listing, for rentals [https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/case-studies/evolve-drives-success-with-flexible-cancellation-policies] (C). | Advertise the exact policy the operator will honour. If there is no free cancellation, do not hint at one. |
| "From" prices? | A low advertised anchor raises intention in general [https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7434854/] (B). A mismatch between the pictured product and the "from" product reads as bait. | The product pictured or described is the product at the price shown. Write "Day tour from NOK 2,350" and name what it buys. |
| Show the price at all? | In Google's data "best" gained ground on "cheap" in considered purchases, which suggests buyers want quality assurance more than the lowest price [https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/9998/Decoding_Decisions_The_Messy_Middle_of_Purchase_Behavior.pdf] (B, a reading of the data). | A high price is a quality signal. Showing it plainly earns trust. Hiding it undermines the signal. |

Experience purchases pay back before departure. Kumar, Killingsworth and Gilovich (2014) found people were happier thinking about an upcoming experiential purchase, with experience sampling of 2,266 adults [https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/08/140825100051.htm] (B). Nawijn and colleagues found vacationers were happier before the trip, and most were not happier after [https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/vacationers-happier-but-most-not-happier-after-a-holiday/] (B). The honest reading: the weeks before count, so "the countdown starts when you book" is a fair line, and "this week will change your life" is not.

Three price lines that work on a feed (fictional brief):

- Total plus per-day: "NOK 18,500 for six days, about NOK 3,083 a day. Two guides and huts included."
- Total plus per-person: "NOK 18,500 per person, sharing a hut room with one other guest." (Only with a real room-sharing rule in the ledger.)
- Deposit plus balance: "Hold a place with NOK 3,000. The rest is due 60 days before you ski."
