# Offer and price: how to build the offer, reverse the risk, frame the price and keep every number honest

What it is: the playbook for the offer line, the risk reducer, price framing for high-ticket experiences, the decision to show or hide price and dates, honest scarcity, and price consistency across image, text, headline and landing page. Read it at method steps 2, 5 and 7: when you build the fact ledger, when you write the price and date lines, and when you run the fact pass.

Every example uses the invented company Brekke Ski Guides. Every example is a fictional brief. Prices, dates, policies, group sizes and inclusions are invented for teaching. Where an example needs a real policy, the line says so, and the real skill asks the user for it.

This file is craft guidance. It is not legal advice. Rules from Norwegian and EU consumer law are summarised from secondary pages and are tagged [VERIFY with counsel].

## 1. Offer construction

An offer has five parts: what you get, the price and its basis, what is included, the terms, and the next step. An ad can leave some of them to the landing page. It must never contradict any of them.

### 1.1 What the canon says

- Hopkins: the best ads ask no one to buy. They offer wanted information and let the customer prove the claims at no cost or risk [PRIMARY: Scientific Advertising, chapter 3, https://archive.org/details/scientific-advertising].
- Hopkins on classes: "an offer limited to a certain class of people is far more effective than a general offer", because people who are entitled to a seeming advantage will go a long way not to lose it [PRIMARY: chapter 6]. In a tour business the class is a level (beginner, advanced), a date (Easter week) or a group (six friends).
- Hopkins on free: "It is hard to pay for an article which has once been free." In his test a paid coupon beat a free sample for the same product [PRIMARY: chapter 6]. Free is not always good.
- Dan Kennedy: match bait to critter, and build an offer the right prospect cannot refuse [SECONDARY: https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/the-3-ms-of-successful-direct-marketing-campaigns-new/313518].
- Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers* (2021): the value equation, dream outcome times perceived likelihood of achievement, divided by time delay times effort and sacrifice. Raise the top two, cut the bottom two [SECONDARY: https://www.selfstorming.com/tools/libraries/frameworks/value-equation and https://www.scalabl.com/literature/100m-offers/]. It is a thinking model and not measured science. A pile of free bonuses can also read cheap for a premium product.
- Harry Dry's teardown of Sky Bet's "Super 6" lists six offer traits: low friction, life integration, social fit, big prize, perceived achievability and free participation [PRIMARY page: https://marketingexamples.com/copywriting/sky-bet]. The cost figures on that page are Dry's own and unaudited [VERIFY]. Use the traits as a lens. Quote no figures.

### 1.2 The four levers, applied (fictional brief)

Use the value equation to decide which lever an ad moves. Name the lever in the receipt.

| Lever | Question | Brekke line (fictional brief) | Ledger fact needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | The result the reader wants by Sunday night | "Your first summit on skis." | The tour reaches a named summit or ridge |
| Perceived likelihood | Why would it work for me? | "Six guests per guide. Avalanche kit lent." | Group cap, kit line |
| Time delay | How soon do I get it? | "Saturdays, January to April. Book this week, ski this month." | Real schedule and booking cut-off |
| Effort and sacrifice | What will it cost me in hassle? | "Boots and skis. We bring the rest." | What is included and what the guest brings |

If an ad moves none of the four, it has no offer. Rewrite it.

### 1.3 Offer patterns that stay honest

| Pattern | Example (fictional brief) | Use when | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain offer | "Two ski days. NOK 4,900. Avalanche kit lent." | Most aware readers | Price and inclusions in the ledger |
| Class limited | "For skiers who are comfortable on red runs." | Any level, to qualify | The requirement is real and stated on the landing page |
| Date limited | "Two departures: 14 and 21 Mar 2027." | Most aware readers | Both dates in the ledger |
| Real extra | "Book by 1 Feb and the airport transfer is on us (worth NOK 450)." | Product aware readers | A real extra, a real deadline, a real value, all in the ledger |
| Ladder | "Try the day first. NOK 2,350. Keep the receipt for the six day trip." | Product aware readers with a doubt | The credit exists in the terms |
| Early price | "NOK 18,500 until 1 Feb, then NOK 19,900." | Any | The change will happen and has a date |

Notes:

- Google's simulation of 31,000 online shoppers found that "power of free" ranked first or second in 18 of 31 categories. A free extra day in car hire pulled 70 percent of shoppers from the favourite [PRIMARY, read in full: https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/9998/Decoding_Decisions_The_Messy_Middle_of_Purchase_Behavior.pdf]. Prefer a real named extra to a percentage discount. The extra must be real, specific and valuable. Never use "free" to hide a higher base price.
- Dan Ariely, a co-author of the original zero-price paper, has had other work retracted. A 2012 honesty-pledge paper was withdrawn in 2021 after its data were shown to be fabricated [SECONDARY: https://bigthink.com/the-present/honesty-pledge-study-fraud-dan-ariely/]. The zero-price effect has independent support in Google's test. Cite Google, and never write "Ariely found".
- Any "was NOK X, now NOK Y" line needs a real prior price and its dates. See section 6.

## 2. Risk reversal

Risk reversal takes the buyer's risk onto you. For a NOK 18,500 trip that is the highest value line a user can hand adquill.

### 2.1 What the canon says

- Hopkins documents the pure form. A motor maker offered a motor for a week "without cost or obligation", and about nine in ten trials led to sales [PRIMARY: chapter 3]. In a parable in chapter 6, a horse seller who said "try it for a week, then pay" beat one who offered a refund [PRIMARY].
<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-start -->
- Hormozi names four guarantee types: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee and implied [SECONDARY: https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary].
<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-end -->
- Cancellation as risk reversal. One Expedia partner case study reported a flexible cancellation policy lifted bookings by 26 percent and revenue by 53 percent per listing. That is a single vendor, about rentals and not guided trips. Treat it as a hypothesis to test [SECONDARY: https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/case-studies/evolve-drives-success-with-flexible-cancellation-policies].

### 2.2 What a guided trip cannot offer

A guided trip cannot be tried for a week and returned. Weather, snow cover and avalanche conditions cannot be promised. So the real risk reducers are different:

1. The cancellation terms, in plain words.
2. What happens when conditions change the plan.
3. A deposit instead of full payment up front.
4. A cheaper first step (the ladder).
5. A level description clear enough that the buyer knows if they can do it.
6. A refund if the operator cancels.

### 2.3 How to write it

Rule: advertise only the exact policy the operator will honour, in plain words. If there is no free cancellation, do not hint at one. Ask the user for the policy once and store it in the ledger.

Fictional brief, with an invented policy:

<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-start -->
- Weak: "Book with confidence." (A claim with no content.)
<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-end -->
- Strong: "Cancel up to 60 days before and we refund you in full. If the avalanche forecast closes a route, the guide picks another." (121 characters)
- Why: it gives a date, a rule and what happens on the mountain. The invented policy here is for teaching. A real ad uses the user's real policy.

What never goes in the ad:

<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-start -->
- "Risk free" with unstated conditions.
- Any guarantee about snow, summit, weather or aurora. Meta's policy risk is highest on unrealistic or guaranteed outcomes [VERIFY: Meta's live policy page. See policy.md].
- A guarantee the operator cannot or will not honour.
- Per-guest "money back if you don't love it" copy unless the policy says so.
<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-end -->

If the ledger has no cancellation or weather policy, write the ad without a risk line and put a question at the top of the output: what is the cancellation policy, and what happens when conditions change the plan?

## 3. Price framing for high-ticket experiences

The goal is clarity first, appeal second. A buyer paying NOK 18,500 will check your number.

### 3.1 Total first

State the total price, what it covers and what it costs to cancel. Per-day, per-person and deposit framing are supplements and never replacements. Mental accounting favours one bundled figure for a treat, and buyers dislike discovering add-ons [SECONDARY: Thaler 1985, Marketing Science, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:4:y:1985:i:3:p:199-214]. Package travel is marketed at a total price, and Norwegian price rules expect the total [SECONDARY, VERIFY with counsel: https://lovdata.no/artikkel/pakkereiser/5166].

### 3.2 "From" prices

A "from" price invites clicks and sets a low anchor. Lab research shows low advertised anchors raise attitude and purchase intention in general [SECONDARY: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7434854/]. The risk is bait: if the ad shows a six day trip and the "from" price belongs to a day tour, buyers feel tricked, and misleading-omission rules apply [VERIFY with counsel for travel].

Rules:

1. The product pictured or described is the product at the price shown.
2. Never write "from" without naming what the price buys. "Intro day from NOK 2,350" works. "From NOK 2,350" does not.
3. The landing page must show that starting price as the starting price.

### 3.3 Per-day framing

Gourville (1998, Journal of Consumer Research) found that "pennies a day" framing raises compliance, because it moves the comparison to small recurring expenses [SECONDARY: https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/24/4/395/1797969]. The effect needs a plausible small-expense comparison, and it invites a hotel-per-night comparison that may flatter or hurt.

Use it when the stay is long and the inclusions are rich. Do the maths in code and show your work. Fictional brief: NOK 18,500 for six days is NOK 3,083.33 a day, so "about NOK 3,083 a day" is a true statement [calculated]. Never show per-day alone. A day tour needs no per-day line.

### 3.4 Anchoring

The first number a reader sees becomes the reference. Tversky and Kahneman showed the pattern in 1974 with a wheel of fortune that stopped at 10 or 65 and shifted estimates [SECONDARY: https://yukaichou.com/behavioral-analysis/anchoring-adjustment-heuristic-decision-design/, paper at https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124]. Effects in real price settings are real but smaller than lab numbers, and weaker for buyers who know the product [SECONDARY: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7434854/]. Intermediate and expert skiers will check your number.

Rules:

1. Real anchors only. An outside anchor needs a source and a date in the copy or on the page. Fictional brief: "A one day lift pass at Example Resort costs NOK 780 (invented figure, checked 1 Oct 2026)."
2. Like for like. A guided day and an unguided lift ticket are different products. Compare only what a buyer would compare.
3. Comparative claims about named competitors carry legal rules [VERIFY with counsel].
4. Pre-suasion helps here. Cialdini's *Pre-Suasion* (2016) argues that what holds attention just before the ask shapes the answer [SECONDARY: https://www.neurosciencemarketing.com/blog/articles/cialdini-7th-unity.htm]. The practical translation is adquill's own: if line one is a ridge at seven in the morning, the price three lines later is judged against that picture. If line one is the price, the trip is judged as a cost.

Decoy pricing (a third tier that makes another look cheap) is low confidence for trip tiers. A 2014 study found it largely vanishes with realistic, non-numeric choices [SECONDARY, VERIFY: no page opened by the research team]. Showing two or three tiers side by side is fine for clarity. Do not engineer a decoy.

### 3.5 The included stack

A stack makes value concrete. It also protects against drip pricing. Hiding mandatory fees until checkout raised revenue by about 21 percent and made buyers 14.1 percent more likely to complete in a StubHub field experiment, and it is a regulatory target [SECONDARY: Blake, Moshary, Sweeney and Tadelis, https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25186/w25186.pdf]. The ethical and legal answer is to show what is and is not included up front.

Write the stack from the ledger only. Fictional brief: "Two guides. Five nights in huts. Breakfasts and dinners. Avalanche kit." If a cost is not included, say so: "You bring skis and boots." That line is a concession and a trust builder.

### 3.6 Deposit and balance

Prelec and Loewenstein (1998) modelled the "pain of paying" and predicted people like to prepay so that consumption feels free [SECONDARY: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.17.1.4]. A small deposit lowers the barrier at first contact. Both hold. State what the deposit is, when the rest is due and what happens if plans change.

Norwegian package travel rules govern price increases, with notice of at least 20 days before departure for an increase to be valid [SECONDARY, grade C, VERIFY with counsel: https://www.travelnetkrs.no/pakkereiseloven]. Instalment and buy-now-pay-later wording changes both psychology and regulation. Do not mention them without counsel [VERIFY with counsel].

### 3.7 Charm endings

Anderson and Simester ran three field experiments and found a "9" price ending raised demand in all three, more for new items [PRIMARY, abstract only: https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2003/effects-of-9-price-endings-on-retail-sales-evidence/]. The abstract gives no percentage, so adquill gives none. For adquill the lesson is narrow: use the price in the ledger, exactly as it is. Never round it or tidy it to look neater.

### 3.8 Price transparency is trust

For considered purchases, buyers lean toward quality assurance over a low price, and showing the total, the inclusions and the policy up front earns an expert's trust that anchoring cannot. A 2023 sweep summarised by Forbrukertilsynet found 148 of 399 online shops tested used manipulative design, 12 of them Norwegian. Regulators are looking at how prices are displayed [SECONDARY: https://netthandel.no/forbrukertilsynet-forklarer-dette-ma-nettbutikkene-unnga-a-gjore/].

## 4. Price line templates

Three templates. Pick by the buyer's question. All use ledger numbers.

| Template | Pattern | Fictional brief example |
|---|---|---|
| Total plus per-day | Total. Per-day. Inclusions | "NOK 18,500 for six days, about NOK 3,083 a day, guides and huts included." (73 characters) |
| Total plus per-person | Per person. Per pair | "NOK 18,500 a person. NOK 37,000 for two." (40 characters) |
| Deposit plus balance | Deposit. Due date | "Hold a place with NOK 3,000. The rest is due 60 days before you ski." (68 characters) |

Check in code: 18,500 times 2 is 37,000. 18,500 divided by 6 is 3,083.33. The deposit and the due date come from the ledger. If the ledger has no deposit, there is no deposit line.

## 5. When to show price and dates, and when to test without

There is no controlled evidence in the research for or against showing price in ads for high-ticket trips. What exists is doctrine, platform behaviour and regulatory pressure. Say so when you advise.

### 5.1 The case for showing

- It qualifies the reader. A high price is itself a signal of quality and commitment, and hiding it can weaken that signal. Insiders read specifics as proof the operator knows the craft [SECONDARY: Berger and Ward 2010, Journal of Consumer Research, https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/37/4/555/1795043].
- It builds trust with expert buyers (section 3.8).
- It lowers misleading-omission risk.
- Warm audiences already know what the thing is and need the date and price to act.

### 5.2 The case for testing without

- A price before the scene lands can make the trip a cost before it is a wish. Pre-suasion is the argument (section 3.4).
- Cold audiences may need the idea before the invoice.

### 5.3 Decision table

| Audience and placement | Price | Dates |
|---|---|---|
| Retargeting, site visitors, email list (product aware, most aware) | Show | Show |
| Cold, low-ticket day tour | Show, and test one version without | Show the season, not a single date |
| Cold, high-ticket multi-day trip | Test with and without in the image. Always show it in the primary text or on the landing page's first screen | Show the season in the image, specific dates in the text |
| Reels and Stories | Put it in on-screen text, never only in a voiceover | Same |
| X native post | Put the total in the post if it is the hook, otherwise put it in the card | Same |

### 5.4 How to run the test

1. Same image, same audience, same budget. One ad shows the price on the image. One ad shows no price.
2. Decide the metric and the stopping rule before launch. Compare cost per landing page view and cost per booking start, and read bookings from the booking system. Click-through rate alone will favour the ad without a price and mislead you.
3. Small budgets cannot detect small differences. Test one big contrast and not five small ones.
4. If the ad hides the price, the landing page shows it on the first screen. Never make the click the only way to learn it.

### 5.5 Dates go stale

- A date on an image is a printed date. Pull the ad when it passes.
- For evergreen campaigns, put the season in the image and the departure dates in the text, so you can edit them without making a new image.
- Advantage+ creative can rewrite text inside an image. Switch it off for any ad with a price or a date, then check every placement preview. meta.md has the checklist.

## 6. Honest scarcity only

True scarcity is a fact with a number, a source and a date. Everything else is pressure.

### 6.1 The scarcity gate

adquill writes a number-based scarcity line only when the brief supplies the number, the source and the date. It never writes a timer. It never writes "only N left" from a guess.

<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-start -->
| Allowed, with proof in the ledger | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| "Six guests per guide on every trip." (a fixed cap) | "Only 2 spots left!" when the booking system shows 5 |
| "Two departures: 14 and 21 Mar 2027." (a count of dates) | A timer that resets on reload |
| "Booking for 14 Mar closes 1 Feb, when the huts are paid for." (a real operating deadline) | A deadline that moves each time someone visits |
| "NOK 18,500 until 1 Feb, then NOK 19,900." (a real, honoured change) | A "special price" that was never lower than the new price |
| "Free cancellation until 1 Feb 2027." (if true) | "Risk free" with unstated conditions |
<!-- adquill-lint: ignore-end -->

Source for the table: the research notes' practical summary of Norwegian and EU rules [SECONDARY, VERIFY with counsel]. A live seat count expires the moment it is read. Prefer fixed facts (a cap, a date) over live counts. If a live count is used, print the date it was read and tell the user to re-check it before publishing.

### 6.2 Why the discipline also pays

Scarcity was most often the least effective of the six levers in Google's tests across 31 categories, and for shoppers still exploring it could "feel restrictive and provoke a negative reaction" [PRIMARY: Google, Decoding Decisions, 2020, https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/9998/Decoding_Decisions_The_Messy_Middle_of_Purchase_Behavior.pdf]. Use it late in the buyer's decision and only when it is true.

## 7. Price consistency across image, text, headline and landing page

On Meta, a price that differs between the ad and the landing page is among the most common rejection reasons in vendor reports. Those summaries say the reviewer opens the landing page and compares [SECONDARY, VERIFY against Meta's live page: https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ad-guidelines]. Meta's own policy index lists relevance requirements: ads must accurately represent offerings and match landing pages [PRIMARY, verified 2026-10-06: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/]. Beyond rejection, a mismatch costs trust. Treat consistency as a hard gate.

### 7.1 The consistency table

Every slot that carries a price, a date or an inclusion gets checked against the ledger and against the landing page value the user pastes.

| Slot | What it may show | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Image lines | Price, dates | Same figure as the ledger, same format, same basis |
| Primary text | Price, dates, inclusions | Same figure. May add the basis ("a person") |
| Headline | Price or offer | The same figure. Does not introduce a new number |
| Description | Inclusions, policy | Must not contradict the primary text |
| CTA line | Real deadline | Must be in the ledger |
| Landing page | Everything | Matches the ad exactly. The user pastes the value, and the skill blocks the output if it differs |

### 7.2 What must match

1. The number. NOK 4,900 everywhere.
2. The format. "NOK 4,900", with a comma for thousands, in every slot.
3. The basis. Per person, per room, per group, twin share. If the ad says "a person", the page says the same.
4. The inclusion list. If the ad says kit lent, the page says kit lent.
5. The dates. The same format, the same departures.
6. The product. The pictured trip is the priced trip.
7. The deposit and the total. A reader must never mistake the deposit for the price.

### 7.3 The check, step by step

1. List every price, date and inclusion in the draft, by slot.
2. Match each to a ledger line ID.
3. Ask the user to paste the live landing page price. If it is missing, ask. Do not guess it.
4. If any figure differs, block the output and report the mismatch.
5. When the price changes later, pull every ad that carries the old figure.

Fictional brief, a mismatch caught:

| Slot | Shows | Ledger | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Image line 3 | NOK 4,900 | NOK 4,900 | Match |
| Headline | Two days, NOK 4,900 | NOK 4,900 | Match |
| Landing page (pasted) | NOK 5,200 including kit hire | NOK 4,900, kit lent | Block. Page and ledger disagree. Ask which is right |
