# 02. Travel, outdoor and adventure creators: voiceover and short scripts

Scope: what documented travel and outdoor creators and destination brands do with voiceover, for a 15 to 25 second short and a 2 to 3 minute YouTube video. Client: Outdoor Norway, Voss, winter ski tours.

Method and honesty notes:
- Interviews with creators about scripting are thin. Most press pieces describe careers, not craft. Where no creator statement exists, I measured their published videos.
- Measurement (my own, 2026-10-07): I pulled English captions for 19 public YouTube videos with yt-dlp and counted words and timings. Captions are YouTube auto speech recognition. Counts include on-camera dialogue and other speakers. Words per second (wps) uses full video duration as the denominator, so it slightly understates the speaking rate. Sample is small. Grade C for every measured number. Six more videos were blocked by YouTube bot checks and are not counted.
- I did not watch the videos frame by frame. Visual notes come from narration cues ("this is", "look at this") and are marked as inferred.
- Grades: A platform official or peer reviewed. B large dataset or documented production document. C practitioner lore, single analyst or my small sample.

Summary (grade C, n=14 creator videos with voice):
- Creator wps median 2.75, range 1.78 to 3.81. Creator shorts median 2.87. Creator long form median 2.55.
- Narrated brand films run slower: 1.35 to 1.82 wps. They leave room for music and picture.
- Every creator short starts speaking at 0.4 s or earlier. 8 to 14 words land in the first 3 seconds.
- Only 1 of 9 sampled creator shorts fits under 25 seconds. The rest run 37 to 122 s. Our 25 s cap is 2 to 5 times tighter than what these creators publish. Their structures must be compressed, not copied.

### 1.3 Word budgets for Outdoor Norway (derived)

Derivation: use the 2.5 wps creator floor, not the 2.87 short median, because a calm guide voice is the brand (07 section 3.2) and faster reads overload. Then reserve visual-only time.

| Format | Hard max | Voice time | Target words | Hard max words | Facts allowed | Beats |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | 25 s | 21 to 23 s (reserve 2 to 4 s picture-only, usually the end card) | 45 to 58 | 62 | 3 hard facts (place, price or duration, one proof) | 4 to 5 beats of 4 to 6 s |
| Long (YouTube 16:9) | 200 s | 160 to 175 s (reserve 25 to 40 s for picture and music breaths) | 380 to 450 | 500 | 1 hard fact per beat, max 12 total | 6 to 8 beats of 20 to 35 s |

Validator logic: words / 2.5 = estimated seconds. Fail if estimate plus reserved silence exceeds the cap. Fail a short over 62 words and a long over 500, whatever the writer claims about pace.

## 2. Rules (testable)

### 2.1 Hook

| ID | Rule | Test | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Short: first spoken word at 0.0 to 0.5 s. No logo, no title card, no "hey guys". | First line of script is VO, not a slate. | All 9 creator shorts measured, 0.0 to 0.4 s (C). TikTok 3 s rule (A). |
| H2 | Short: the hook sentence is 6 to 12 words and states what the video is about. | Count words before first full stop. | 8 to 14 words in first 3 s in creator shorts (C). |
| H3 | Long: the title promise is visible or spoken within 30 s. | Script beat 1 ends by 30 s (75 words) and restates the title promise. | YouTube Intro metric (A). |
| H4 | Hook names a concrete place or thing, not a mood. | Hook contains a proper noun or a number. | Measured hooks: "Svalbard", "Patagonia", "remote Afghan village", "America's most expensive grocery store" (C). |
| H5 | No greeting opener in either format. | Fail "welcome to", "what's up guys", "hey everyone" in the first 10 words. | Drew Binsky's 2019 long videos opened with "what's up guys" and "welcome to"; his 2026 shorts open on the claim. The creators dropped it themselves (C). |

### 2.2 Density and facts

| ID | Rule | Test |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | One hard fact per sentence. A hard fact is a price, a number, a date, a duration or a named place. | Count numerals and proper nouns per sentence. Fail at 2 or more, except a place name plus its one number ("Voss, an hour from Bergen"). |
| D2 | Short: max 3 hard facts in the whole script. | Count. |
| D3 | Long: max 12 hard facts, max 2 per beat. | Count per beat. |
| D4 | Every fact must be pointable. It needs a matching shot in the visual column. | Each fact row has a non-empty visual cell. |
| D5 | Itinerary chains (A then B then C) are allowed only as an obstacle or a joke, and max 1 per script. | Fail 3 or more place names in one sentence unless the next line is a consequence. Kara and Nate fit a 5-stop flight chain into 13 s because it ends in "where I left my passport on the plane" (C). |
| D6 | Lists are allowed only when the list itself is the payoff, each item gets its own shot, and items stay under 3 s each. | Drew Binsky's grocery short reads 10 prices in about 25 s, one per product shot. Works because prices are the topic. A list of activities with no payoff fails. |
| D7 | Price goes late. If price is the hook, open the loop early ("the cost might surprise you") and pay it in the last 15% of runtime. | Lost LeBlanc Amazon short: promise at 10 s, "$14,000" at 101 s of 111 s (C). |
| D8 | On-screen text max 5 words per caption card on a short. TikTok's ceiling is 5 to 10 words per second (A); for a calm brand and a 25 s cap, use the floor. | Count words per text overlay. |

### 2.3 Voice and POV

| ID | Rule | Test |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Default to "you" for guest action and "we" for guide action. No "Outdoor Norway offers". | String match. From the client voice rules (07 section 3.3). |
| V2 | Creator first person ("I tried", "my friends and I") does not transfer to brand VO, unless a named guide is the narrator on camera. | If script uses "I", a named guide must be listed as narrator. |
| V3 | Third person character story is allowed for brand films. Visit Norway's family film uses a narrator and five characters, each with one want in one line. | If third person, each character gets one want in 12 words or fewer. |
| V4 | Present tense. Creator shorts narrate in present or simple past ("tonight we are staying", "we cooled off"). No future-sales tense ("you will experience"). | Fail "you will experience", "you'll discover", "get ready to". |
| V5 | No superlative without a source. Creators use "most unique hotel in the world" and "world's best". Outdoor Norway's content-qc bans unsupported superlatives. | Fail "best", "most", "only", "world's" unless a source is attached. |
| V6 | No fear or negative hook. Lost LeBlanc's "You will die in the next 2 hours" works for him, but the client rules ban negative framing of activities. | Fail death, danger, "if you can handle it". |

### 2.4 Structure

| ID | Rule | Test |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Short: 4 to 5 beats. Hook (0 to 3 s), turn or proof (3 to 10 s), payoff shot (10 to 18 s), close plus CTA (18 to 25 s). | Beat timestamps present, each beat 4 to 7 s. |
| S2 | Short: one loop only. Open in the hook, close in the last beat. No supporting loops. 25 s cannot carry nested loops. | Count open questions. Max 1. |
| S3 | Long: one dominant question opened by 30 s, closed in the final 20 s. Partial payoffs at least every 40 s. | Each beat ends with a payoff or a new specific question. Nas Daily's "Why this country is so happy" asks people on the street in the first seconds and answers in pieces (each interview) before the closing line "happiness is equality" (C). |
| S4 | Long: an energy reset every 30 to 45 s (new location, new person, a reaction line, a music break). | Beat list shows a change of scene, speaker or sound at least every 45 s. |
| S5 | Close on an image or a felt line, then the CTA. Never end on a fact list. | Last VO line has no numeral, except a price close (D7). |

### 2.5 Call to action

| ID | Rule | Test |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | One CTA only. | Count imperative verbs in the close. Max 1 CTA verb. |
| C2 | Prefer a share or question CTA on shorts; a booking CTA belongs in caption or end card. | Kara and Nate: "What do you think? Was the journey worth it? Send this to a friend who would travel to the bottom of the world just to stay here." (C) |
| C3 | Keyword comment CTAs ("comment Anaconda") only if a DM automation exists. | [VERIFY: does Outdoor Norway run comment-to-DM automation?] |
| C4 | Signature sign-offs ("That's one minute, see you tomorrow", "I'm Drew Binsky and if you like my travel videos please click subscribe") are a creator brand asset. They cost 3 to 5 s. Not for a 25 s brand short. | Fail sign-offs over 8 words on a short. |

## 3. Creator and brand profiles

Facts per beat and POV are from transcripts unless noted. Visual notes are inferred.

### 3.1 Nas Daily (Nuseir Yassin)
- Structure: 60 s historically; 2 to 4 min now. "Secret halal country" short: claim (0 to 8 s), proof by pointing ("This is halal" three times), place reveal at 11 s, benefits, a lab as the surprising proof, moral close.
- Hook: counter-intuitive claim plus number ("over 160,000 halal products"; "a country where almost every car is a Tesla").
- Facts: about one number per 20 to 30 s. The rest is "this is" pointing. First person host. 2.34 to 2.57 wps.
- Close: a one-line moral ("Happiness is equality."). Does not transfer: the host persona.

### 3.2 Drew Binsky
- Structure: 2019 long form (157 to 230 s) is greeting, location definition, fact tour, 25-word subscribe sign-off. 2026 shorts (37 to 43 s) are claim, evidence montage, a person's line as the close.
- Hook: "This kid speaks five languages fluently... but can't afford to buy food." Contrast in under 6 s. First person, 2.74 to 3.11 wps.
- Lesson: his own move from "what's up guys" (2019) to claim-first openers (2026) is the cleanest evidence that greeting openers were dropped.

### 3.3 Kara and Nate
- 88 s short: hedged claim ("what might be the most unique hotel in the world"), journey as obstacle (5-stop flight chain ending "where I left my passport on the plane"), arrival, room tour in three levels, sensory problem (100 km/h wind, "the whole thing shaking"), honest downside ("didn't get very much sleep"), question close plus share CTA.
- 5 numbers in 88 s, each tied to a visible thing. "We" couple voice. 2.87 to 3.81 wps, the fastest in the set, carried by fast cuts. Sampled shorts: 1.4M and 2.8M views.
- Transfers: name the downside before the verdict. That is why the close reads as honest, not as an ad.

### 3.4 Mark Wiens
- Hook: "Sizzling sisig is probably the world's best Filipino street food. We're at the source in Pampanga and the process will make your mouth water." Sensory promise plus place. 2.71 to 2.76 wps.
- Selling: "I occasionally have done some paid-for sponsorships, but not very often." Ads on all videos are his biggest earner. "I never wanted to be a host." (Entrepreneur, B)
- Transfers: the sensory promise (the waffle at the mountain café). Does not: eating reactions as payoff.

### 3.5 Lost LeBlanc
- Two modes: music montage (Norway road trip, 250 s, first word at 30.5 s) and VO-led shorts (21 to 111 s).
- Amazon short: stakes hook, price loop opened at 10 s ("the cost might surprise you"), "by day" montage, ritual, "later that night" set piece, verdict ("I've been to 100 countries now..."), price paid at 101 s ("$14,000"), comment-keyword CTA, "Get out there and see it for yourself".
- Transfers: the delayed price loop and the "by day, later that night" frame, a day structure without an itinerary.

### 3.6 Visit Norway, winter
- "Norway in Winter: Family-Friendly Holiday Ideas" (Oct 2025, 112 s, 614k views, 1.82 wps): third person narrator. "Meet the Hendersons." Five characters, one want each in one line ("dad just wanted to hear silence for once"). Conflict line. Payoff as a verb list: "They sled it, built it, threw it, rafted it, danced it, skied it, fished it, walked it." 8 activities in about 8 s. Close: "Because tomorrow is another glorious snowy day."
- Lesson: an activity list works as rhythmic verbs after a want is set up. A noun list ("skiing, snowshoeing, dog sledding") does not.
- Ski clips run 57 to 72 s ("Ski touring in Norway with a guide", "best backpack"). [VERIFY: captions blocked, watch for structure.]
- Innovation Norway commissioned a ski-touring safety film with avalanche forecaster Markus Landrø of Varsom.no. A guide line about conditions is part of the official winter story.

### 3.7 Inspired by Iceland
- Ask Gudmundur (B): locals named Gudmundur answer viewers' questions. Results in 1.2. Mission: Iceland (101 s) is sparse VO at 1.35 wps.
- Transfers: a named Voss guide answers one real guest question. Does not: topical parody.

### 3.8 Visit Finland, Rent a Finn (B)
- The case names the category cliché as Norway's: "Fjords. Mountains. Midnight sun. Picturesque fishermen's cottages. Northern Lights. Hygge... all the things Norway and Sweden have been advertising for years." Their fix: sell a person and a feeling, not scenery.

### 3.9 Patagonia and The Fifty (long-form outdoor)
- Skyword (C): action over products, "If your products are at the center of your content, you're doing it wrong." Cody Townsend's ski series runs 15 to 23 min.
- Transfers: "verbs before nouns", people doing things on snow. Does not transfer: length, 5 to 10 times our cap.

Samuel and Audrey (2013 Helsinki vlog, 1.78 wps, gaps up to 34 s, close "we're both really tired") is the unscripted baseline: no hook, no loop, no fact plan.

## 4. Correcting the existing script-writing skill

| Existing rule | Verdict | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant loop opened in hook, closed at the very end | Holds for 25 s (one loop only). Holds for 200 s only with partial payoffs. | Short: one loop, no supporting loops (S2). Long: dominant question closed in last 20 s, but pay a partial answer at least every 40 s (S3). Never withhold the title promise past 30 s (H3). |
| Supporting loops in between | Too many for a short. | Short: zero. Long: max 3, each closed within 60 s of opening. |
| Zeigarnik effect | Works as a price loop (Lost LeBlanc). | Allow "price late" pattern (D7). Do not tease a natural phenomenon you cannot guarantee (aurora, powder). |
| Every word earns its place | Right, but not testable. | Replace with budgets: 62 words max short, 500 words max long, wps 2.5 (1.3). |
| Continuous prose output | Wrong for video. | Output a two-column table: time, VO, visual, on-screen text. Validator reads it. |
| No CTA | Missing. | One CTA (C1). Share or question on shorts, booking on end card. |
| No brand or facts rules | Missing. | V1, V5, V6 plus fact sourcing against the claims bank (07 section 7). |

## 5. What makes travel scripts fail, and the fix

| Failure | Example pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greeting opener | "Hey guys, welcome to Voss" | Spends the 3 s proposition window (A) on nothing. | Open on the claim: "The gondola leaves from the train platform." [VERIFY against claims bank: Voss gondola start] |
| Scenery noun list | "Fjords, mountains, northern lights" | The category default. Visit Finland named it as Norway's cliché. | One specific image with a sound or taste. |
| Itinerary reading | "Day 1 Bergen, day 2 Voss, day 3 Myrkdalen" | Many facts, no stake. Overloads. | Time frame instead of dates: "by day... after the lifts close". Max 1 chain, as an obstacle (D5). |
| Cliché adjectives | breathtaking, magical, epic, unforgettable, winter wonderland | Banned by client rubric. Says nothing. | Name the thing. "Dry snow squeaks under the skins." |
| Fact stacking | "9 days, 4 resorts, NOK 42,126, small groups, certified guides" in one breath | 5 facts in 4 s. | One fact per sentence, one per shot (D1, D4). |
| Future-sales tense | "You will experience the adventure of a lifetime" | Reads as an ad. | Present tense, what happens: "You skin up at nine." |
| Unhedged promise | "Perfect powder", "see the aurora" | Breaks content-qc. | Possibility verbs: "look for", "if the sky clears". |
| Narration that ignores the picture | VO describes what is already obvious on screen | Doubles the load. | VO adds what the picture cannot say: the cost, the name, the reason. |
| Long sign-off | 25-word subscribe line | 10 s of a 25 s short. | One-line CTA, end card does the rest. |

## 6. What does not transfer to Outdoor Norway

- Creator short length (37 to 122 s). Copy the hook and close, not the middle.
- 3.8 wps speed. A guide voice at that rate overloads. Budget at 2.5.
- First person host ("I tried") without a recurring face. Use "you" and "we", or a named guide.
- Superlatives and fear hooks. Banned by client rules.
- Price-list payoffs. A tour has one price.
- 15 to 40 minute documentary structure. Keep "people doing things", drop the length.
- Comment-keyword CTA without automation [VERIFY]. Parody stunts that need a news hook.
