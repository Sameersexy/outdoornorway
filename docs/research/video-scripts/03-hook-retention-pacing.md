# 03 Hook, retention, pacing and information density by length

Research date: 2026-10-07. Scope: Outdoor Norway voiceover videos. Two tiers only.
- SHORT: Reels, Shorts, TikTok. Hard max 25 s.
- LONG: YouTube landscape. Hard max 200 s (3:20).

Grades: A = platform official or peer reviewed. B = large dataset, platform research summarised by trade press, or documented creator process. C = practitioner lore or single case. J = our judgement, derived from the sources named next to it. [VERIFY] = not confirmed at source this session.

Key finding up front: no platform publishes a universal retention threshold. Bytecap's 2026 evidence review of YouTube Help pages says so directly. Our limits are therefore derived from speech rate, reading speed and working memory research, which are measurable, and then fitted to the platform facts below.

## 3. Speaking pace

| Context | Words per minute | Words per second | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Audiobook narration industry default | 150-160 (155 most cited) | 2.5-2.7 | sites.google.com/view/snowdaycaculators/audiobook-narration-speed-explained-what-150-160-wpm-really-means | C |
| Radio and podcast hosts | 150-160 | 2.5-2.7 | virtualspeech.com/blog/average-speaking-rate-words-per-minute | C |
| Presentations, comfortable | 100-150 | 1.7-2.5 | same | C |
| US conversational average | about 150 | 2.5 | same, citing National Center for Voice and Speech | B |
| TED talks, 5 samples | 154-201, mean 173 | 2.6-3.4 | same | C (n=5) |
| British English 1990 vs 2020 | about 170 rising to 198 | 2.8 to 3.3 | francis-press.com/papers/4225 (198 is "17 percent faster by 28 words" than Tauroza and Allison 1990) | B |
| Radio guidance | 160-180 | 2.7-3.0 | same search set | C |
| Comprehension drops for most listeners | above 180 | above 3.0 | audiobook source above | C [VERIFY] |
| Azure neural TTS, en-US SaraNeural default | 157 | 2.6 | tessl.io registry, azure-text-to-speech skill | C |
| Non-native listeners | Slower rates aid comprehension. Griffiths 1992, TESOL Quarterly 26(2) 385-390 | n/a | cited in jalt-publications.org | A study exists. [VERIFY: exact wpm, often quoted as about 127 vs 188 wpm] |

Our working rates (J):

| Style | Raw wpm | Raw wps |
|---|---|---|
| Energetic short-form native | 165-180 | 2.75-3.0 |
| Natural, not rushed | 145-160 | 2.4-2.65 |
| Documentary narration | 130-150 | 2.2-2.5 |
| Non-native narrator (guides at Outdoor Norway) | 120-140 | 2.0-2.3 |

Effective words per second, accounting for breaths, pauses, a music sting and a silent final beat (J):
- SHORT 25 s: assume 2.75 raw wps, minus about 15 percent for pauses and the loop beat. Effective 2.3-2.4 wps. 25 x 2.4 = 60 words hard max. Target 45-55 leaves room for one breath beat.
- LONG 200 s: assume 2.4 raw wps, minus 10-25 percent for music beds, natural sound and landscape holds. Effective 1.8-2.2 wps. 200 x 2.2 = 440 words hard max. Target 320-400.

What a TTS narrator changes:
- Rate is fixed and predictable, so word count converts to time cleanly. Validator can trust words / 2.6 = seconds at default speed.
- TTS does not add natural pauses. Write them in (line breaks, SSML breaks) or the script will feel rushed even inside budget.
- Numbers, Norwegian names (Voss, Myrkdalen, Stølsheimen) and units need phonetic checks. Each mispronounced name costs a retake. Keep place names to max 2 per short.

What a non-native narrator changes:
- Natural rate is lower and clusters of consonants slow delivery. Cut budgets by 15 percent: SHORT 50 words, LONG 380 words.
- Avoid idioms and tongue twisters. Prefer 1-2 syllable words.
- The validator should take narrator type as an input and pick the budget.

## 4. Hook windows

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile feed users spend an average 1.7 s on a piece of content. On mobile, 47 percent of a video's value is delivered in the first 3 s, 74 percent by 10 s | Facebook IQ, 2016, via marketingdive.com/news/facebook-why-mobile-video-ads-must-work-fast/446217/ | B (platform research, old, Facebook feed not Reels) |
| TikTok guidance: introduce the proposition in the first 3 s, prioritise the hook in the first 6 s | nestscale.com/blog/tiktok-creative-practices.html | C [VERIFY in TikTok Creative Center] |
| YouTube measures the intro as the first 30 s, and a weak intro is fixed by matching thumbnail and title or changing the first 30 s | support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415 | A |
| MrBeast team: "we freak out so much about the first minute." After the hook, "stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them." Re-engagement segments around minutes 3 and 6 | alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/ | B |
| Retaining viewers for the first half predicts finishing: "If we can get them to watch the first half of the video there's a very high chance they'll watch to the end" | same | B |

Hook rules (J from the above):
- SHORT: the hook is done by 3 s. At 2.75 wps that is 8 words. Allow 4-9, hard max 10. The first frame already shows the payoff subject (skier mid-turn, summit view, guide's face), never a logo, title card or black.
- LONG: verbal hook by 8 s (max 22 words). By 30 s the viewer must know the question the video answers and see proof it will be answered. Max 70 words before the first real content. No channel intro.
- First frame for both: motion already happening, one clear subject, high contrast, text overlay of 3-6 words that states the question or stakes. Must read without sound: Facebook IQ found most feed video needed sound to be understood (24 percent of 850 videos understood without sound), so the frame and overlay carry the hook alone. B.

Hook types with documented support:
- Question or curiosity gap that the video answers. Curiosity improves memory for the answer and for incidental material shown during the curious state (Kang et al 2009; Gruber, Gelman and Ranganath 2014, Neuron, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/). A.
- Show the outcome first, then how (MrBeast: thumbnail promise met in first minute). B.
- Direct benefit statement. C (opus.pro, stackinfluence.com).
- Pattern interrupt visual. C.
Ban: hooks that the video does not pay off. The YouTube intro metric punishes title or thumbnail mismatch (A).

## 5. Information density

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Working memory focus of attention holds about 4 chunks, range 3-5 in young adults | Cowan 2001, Behavioral and Brain Sciences, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/ ; Cowan 2010, Current Directions, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2864034 | A |
| People learn better when narrated material is broken into segments | Mayer segmenting principle, ugc.futurelearn.com (Mayer 2020 summary) | A |
| Identical on-screen text plus narration impairs learning. Short text that highlights key words helps. A small discrepancy between narration and text can help | Mayer redundancy principle; Yue, Bjork and Bjork 2013, sites.lifesci.ucla.edu (pdf) | A |
| Broadcast writing: one idea per sentence, under 20 words, often 8-12 | us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/tips-for-writing-for-broadcast | C |

Why spoken density must be lower than print: listeners cannot re-read. Spoken items enter working memory one at a time and decay unless chunked. A beat that adds a price, a date, a place name and an altitude is four chunks and fills the store. Nothing else lands.

Density rules (J):
- One new idea per sentence. One new fact per short beat.
- A "fact" = any number, price, date, proper name, statistic or technical term the viewer must remember. Repeating an earlier fact is free.
- SHORT: max 3 facts total, max 2 of them numbers. One takeaway only.
- LONG: max 2 facts per beat, max 4 per chapter, max 14 per script, max 6 numbers. Each chapter ends with a one-line recap or payoff, which re-chunks what came before.
- Never stack two numbers in one sentence ("3 days, 1,200 metres" fails).
- Numbers the viewer needs later (price, dates) go on screen as well as in voice.

## 6. Beats and visual cadence

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Average shot length in English-language films fell from about 12 s (1930s) to about 2.5 s (2010) | Cutting et al 2011, i-Perception, "Quicker, faster, darker", via flowingdata.com/2014/09/22/evolution-of-movies/ | A (peer reviewed) |
| Re-engagement content around minutes 3 and 6 in 10-plus minute videos | MrBeast memo | B |
| YouTube chapters need first timestamp 0:00, at least 3 chapters, each at least 10 s | sendshort.ai/guides/youtube-chapters/ ; wpmanageninja.com | B [VERIFY on support.google.com] |
| Short-form editors cut every 1-3 s | blitzcutai.com and similar | C |

Cadence rules (J):
- SHORT: 4-6 beats of 3-6 s. Visual change every 1.5-3 s. No static shot over 4 s. One beat = one sentence or two short ones.
- LONG: 4-6 chapters of 25-50 s. Each chapter has 3-4 beats of 8-15 s. Shot change every 3-6 s (landscape holds up to 8 s are fine when voice is silent). Pattern interrupt every 20-40 s: new location, guide on camera, map graphic, natural sound break, or a question.
- Scale MrBeast's 3 and 6 minute re-engagements to our 200 s: re-engage at about 60 s and 120 s (the two places a 200 s curve usually dips). J.
- Mark chapters in the script so the editor can add YouTube chapters (needs 3 or more, each 10 s or longer).

Beat count check: 25 s / 5 s = 5 beats. 200 s / 11 s = 18 beats. Ranges in the tier table bracket these.

## 7. On-screen text

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Netflix English subtitles: 42 characters per line, max 2 lines, up to 20 characters per second for adult content | partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977 | A (industry standard) |
| Netflix timing: min 5/6 s per event, max 7 s | Netflix Subtitle Timing Guidelines, partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051554394 | B [VERIFY: seen in search summary, not fetched] |
| Overlays that repeat the narration word for word hurt learning; short keyword overlays help | Mayer, Yue Bjork Bjork 2013 | A |
| TikTok: "Overlay concise and informative text", "include closed captions" | TikTok 7 Top Tips pdf | A |

Rules (J):
- Two kinds of text, kept apart. Captions = full transcript, auto or burned in, bottom of safe box, max 2 lines. Overlays = keyword text that adds or highlights, not repeats.
- Overlay size: SHORT max 6 words or 32 characters. LONG max 8 words, 42 characters per line.
- Overlay duration: at least characters / 15 seconds (slower than Netflix's 20 because the viewer is also watching footage and listening), min 1.5 s short, 2 s long, max 7 s.
- Max 1 overlay on screen at once. Never an overlay and a fast cut in the same 1 s.
- In the script, the visual column holds overlay text verbatim so the validator can count it.

## 8. Call to action

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Checklist: "Does the video include a clear call to action? Does the CTA align with your ad objective?" | TikTok 7 Top Tips pdf | A |
| Sends per reach is a top Instagram metric | socialmediatoday.com (Mosseri) | B |
| YouTube end screens sit in the last 5-20 s of a video | support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789 | [VERIFY: from memory, not fetched] |
| Seamless loop raises replays and watch time | videobgremover.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices | C |

Rules (J):
- SHORT: exactly 1 CTA. Last 4 s. Max 8 words. One verb from an allowed list: Book, Save, Send, Follow, Comment, Ask. "Send this to the friend who..." targets sends per reach. "Book" only when the caption or link carries dates and price.
- SHORT loop-back: the last line should run into the first frame or first line, so a replay feels continuous. Example: end "...and that's why we start at six." First line "Why we start at six." The CTA then lives in the overlay or caption, not the final spoken words, so it does not break the loop. Choose one per short: spoken CTA or loop.
- LONG: 1 primary CTA in the last 20 s, max 25 words, one verb. Optional 1 soft mid-roll CTA after the first payoff (about 60-90 s), max 10 words. No CTA in the first 30 s.
- No stacked asks ("like, subscribe, comment and book") anywhere. Count CTA verbs; more than one in a sentence fails.

## 9. Dopamine and Zeigarnik: honest check

| Claim used by script skills | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Unfinished items are remembered better (Zeigarnik) | Ghibellini and Meier 2025 meta-analysis, Humanities and Social Sciences Communications: 59 studies, recall ratio interrupted vs completed 0.99, d = 0.15. "No memory advantage for unfinished tasks." ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html | Not supported. A |
| People tend to resume interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina) | Same meta-analysis found "a general tendency to resume tasks" | Supported. A. This is the honest basis for open loops: a wish to finish, not better memory |
| Curiosity helps memory and engages reward circuits | Gruber et al 2014: better memory for answers and for incidental material during high curiosity, more hippocampus activity and hippocampus-dopamine circuit interaction. Kang et al 2009: curiosity linked to caudate activity and better recall 11-16 days later | Supported. A |
| Each cut or reveal gives a "dopamine hit" that keeps viewers watching | No source found. Dopamine signals reward prediction and learning, not "pleasure per cut" | Pop neuroscience. Remove from the skill |
| Hold the main answer until the very end in a 3 min video | No direct study. Counter-evidence: Mayer segmenting and signalling (A) favour clear segments; YouTube "dips" mark where viewers leave (A); MrBeast plans re-engagement payoffs at intervals and says first half predicts finish (B); chapters let viewers jump (B) | Risky for LONG. Fine for SHORT |

What to change in the existing skill:
- Keep loops, but justify them with curiosity and the Ovsiankina resumption tendency. Delete Zeigarnik memory claims and any "dopamine" language.
- SHORT (25 s): one dominant loop, opened in the first 3 s, closed in the last beat. No supporting loops: there is no time to close them. A short with 2 open questions breaks the 1 idea rule.
- LONG (200 s): one dominant question, opened by 8 s. Each chapter opens a small question and closes it inside the chapter (mini-payoff), then hands forward with a progress marker ("That's the gear. Now the route."). Give a partial answer to the dominant question by the midpoint (about 100 s) and the full answer in the final chapter. Never more than 2 loops open at once (Cowan, J).
- Add progress markers at each chapter seam. They act as Mayer signalling cues and give the viewer a reason to stay through the likely dip points at 60 s and 120 s.

## 10. What to add to the script-writing skill

1. Length tiers with word budgets from the tier table, adjusted by narrator type (native, non-native, TTS).
2. A two-column output: VO | VISUAL (shot, overlay text verbatim, seconds). Keep continuous prose only as an optional read-through.
3. A beat structure with timestamps so the validator can compute words per beat and seconds per beat.
4. A fact counter: tag each number, name, price or date.
5. Hook spec: seconds, words, first-frame description, sound-off overlay.
6. CTA spec: count, position, verb list, loop-back option for shorts.
7. Facts rule: every price, date, altitude or duration must come from a named source (see the fact-check skill). Brand voice from note 07 section 3.
8. Corrections: remove Zeigarnik memory claim and dopamine framing; replace "never resolve until the end" with chapter mini-payoffs for LONG.
