# 01. What voiceover-led and educational creators do (outside travel)

Scope: two formats only. SHORT = Reels, Shorts, TikTok, hard max 25 s. LONG = YouTube landscape, hard max 200 s (3:20).
Grades: A = platform official or peer reviewed. B = large dataset or documented creator process. C = practitioner lore or single case. D = my derivation from A to C sources (a design choice for the validator, not a finding).
Date of research: 2026-10-07. Fetched pages were treated as data.

Honest headline: almost no creator publishes words per second or facts per 30 seconds. The hard numbers come from one peer-reviewed dataset (Guo et al. 2014), platform docs (TikTok, YouTube), the MrBeast production memo and voiceover timing guides. The word budgets below are derived from those, and are marked D.

## 2. Derived budgets for the validator (grade D)

Base rate 2.6 words per second (N1 mean, matches N4). Keep about 10 percent of runtime for silence, sound and breath (N4, N5 advice to write under the limit).

| Item | SHORT (25 s hard max) | LONG (200 s hard max) |
|---|---|---|
| Spoken words, hard max | 60 | 470 |
| Spoken words, target | 40 to 55 | 380 to 450 |
| 15 s cut-down, hard max | 38 | n/a |
| Spoken hook | 8 words max, inside 3 s (N6) | Promise stated by 15 s (40 words max). Set-up finished by 30 s (75 words max) (N8) |
| Core ideas | 1 | 1 dominant question, 3 to 4 segments |
| New facts (any checkable claim) | 3 max in total | 3 max per 30 s window, 18 max in total |
| Numbers spoken | 2 max in total, 1 per sentence | 1 per sentence, 8 max in total |
| Payoff points | 1 (at 18 to 23 s) | 1 per segment, every 40 to 60 s (N11 scaled to a 200 s video) plus the dominant payoff |
| CTA | 1, 12 words max, final 5 s | 1, 25 words max, final 15 s. Optional 1 soft mention mid-video only if it is content (see R14) |
| Sentence length | 12 words max | 20 words max, average 14 or less |
| On-screen text card | 7 words max per card, keywords only | 7 words max per card, keywords only |

Validator time estimate (D): est_seconds = words / 2.6 + 0.4 per spoken number or proper noun + explicit [PAUSE n s] markers. Fail if est_seconds exceeds 25 or 200. Warn if over 23 or 190. N4 notes that technical terms, names and numbers slow delivery, hence the 0.4 s penalty [VERIFY: calibrate by timing 5 real reads by the Outdoor Norway voice].

Why 2.6 and not 3.0: Guo found faster speech more engaging, but with slides that repeated the content (N2). Outdoor footage carries no text, so a viewer has only the voice. Use 2.6 as the budget. A read can run faster on delivery; the script must not need it.

## 3. Rules (testable)

R1. Hook in the first sentence. Short: first sentence 8 words or fewer and must contain the stake or the question (N6). Long: by word 40 the viewer knows what they will get (N8). Fail test: first sentence over limit, or opens with brand name, greeting or "In this video".

R2. Frame 1 is a visual hook. Every script has a VISUAL column. Row 1 must describe motion or a person, not a logo or title card (N6, TikTok "avoid opening with branding" is reported by practitioners, C). Fail test: visual row 1 empty or contains "logo".

R3. Thumbnail and title promise matches the first 15 s. MrBeast: "The title and thumbnail ... set the expectations" (N9). Fail test: the script declares a promise field and the first 40 words do not contain its key noun.

R4. One idea per short. One question per long. Count idea markers (D). A short with two "Also" or "Plus" pivots fails.

R5. Fact budget per window (section 2). Each checkable claim is tagged [F] with a source line from the fact sheet. Fail if the count in any 30 s window exceeds 3, or any [F] lacks a source.

R6. Misconception or tension first, explanation second (N12). Clear, smooth explanation alone does not teach and is easy to scroll past. Each segment opens with a belief or a problem, then resolves it. Brand constraint: frame as a surprise, never as a warning (client rule: no negative framing).

R7. Long form is a chain of units, not one loop. Hormozi: long form is "how many content units you string together", each unit hook, retain, reward (C, secondary summaries of $100M Leads, [VERIFY: page in book]). Blackman: set up, tension, payoff every 1 to 2 min (N11). Rule: each segment of 40 to 60 s opens a small question and closes it. The dominant question stays open until the final 20 s.

R8. Escalate, do not plateau. MrBeast "stair stepping": each step bigger than the last, best payoff at the end (N10 context). Rule: segments are ordered so stakes or beauty increase. The strongest shot is reserved for the payoff.

R9. Re-engage at the 1/3 mark and 2/3 mark of the long video (about 65 s and 130 s). MrBeast places re-engagements at about 3 and 6 min of 13 (N10). Scaled to 200 s (D). Fail test: no marked [RE-ENGAGE] beat between 55 to 80 s and 120 to 145 s.

R10. Plain words. MrBeast: "for 50 million people to understand something it must be simple" (B). Max one term of jargon per segment, defined in the same sentence (D).

R11. Signal structure (N14). Long form may name the segments ("Three things decide your first tour day"). Shorts do not need it.

R12. Do not duplicate narration as full on-screen text over graphics (N13). Burned-in captions are an accessibility and sound-off layer, written as a separate track. Overlay cards carry keywords or numbers only, 7 words max. [VERIFY: Mayer's redundancy effect was measured in learning tasks, not social feeds; captions for sound-off viewing are standard practice. Keep both, separate them.]

R13. No end signal before the payoff. MrBeast: "Never signal the end of the video unless it's to build hype for the prize or payoff" and "endings must always be abrupt" (B). Ban: "That's it", "To wrap up", "Thanks for watching", "In conclusion" before the last line.

R14. The CTA is content. MrBeast: "BRAND DEALS ARE CONTENT", the integrated style made a shallower retention dip (B). Rule: the CTA names the next concrete step tied to the payoff ("The day starts at the gondola at nine. Link below."). One CTA only.

R15. Loop or ritual ending is allowed. Nas Daily closed every video with the same line (N18). A short may end on a line that sends the viewer back to frame 1. Fail test: none, this is optional.

R16. Every script ships with a fact sheet. Kurzgesagt publishes sources and uses expert review (N16). Hank Green paused several channels in August 2026 after viewers found he had read AI-generated research notes on air; his words: "relying too much on generated notes" (C, Express Tribune and others). For an AI writer this is the strongest rule: no fact enters the script unless it exists in the client fact sheet or a cited page.

R17. Write to the shot list. Johnny Harris carried his outline on location to capture "the right visuals" (N17). Each script row names a shot that exists or can be filmed. Fail test: a visual cell that describes something not in the asset list, without a [SHOOT] flag.

R18. Novelty, not cut speed. Mario Joos (former MrBeast retention editor): "It's not the cutting speed, it's the speed at which you introduce progressive novel information" (C, interview summary). Rule: a new visual every sentence. Default max shot length 4 s in a short and 8 s in a long. [VERIFY: no A or B number exists for cut cadence; tune with Outdoor Norway retention graphs.]

## 4. What to add to and correct in the existing script-writing skill

| Existing idea | Verdict | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant loop opened in the hook, closed at the end | Holds for 25 s. Partly wrong for 200 s | Keep the dominant loop. Add paid-off sub-loops every 40 to 60 s (R7, N11). A single unresolved question for 3 minutes, with nothing paid off, is the "plateau" MrBeast fights with re-engagements. Close the dominant loop in the last 20 s, not the last line, so the CTA has room |
| Zeigarnik effect | Usable as framing, weak as evidence | Treat as C. The large-scale memory effect replicates poorly in the literature [VERIFY: see 02-psychology-influence.md for the grade used there]. The working mechanism is curiosity gap plus payoff, which N11 and N10 support |
| "Every word earns its place" | Correct but untestable | Replace with word budgets, fact budgets and sentence caps (section 2) |
| Continuous prose output | Wrong for video | Output a two-column table: TIME, VO, VISUAL, ON-SCREEN TEXT, [F] source. Prose cannot be timed or shot |
| No length tiers | Gap | Two tiers only: SHORT 25 s, LONG 200 s, with a 15 s cut-down flag for shorts |
| No density limit | Gap | Ideas, facts and numbers per window (R4, R5) |
| No brand or facts rules | Gap | R16 plus client voice rules (07, section 3): "you" and "we", sensory specifics, no superlatives, no guaranteed snow or aurora |
| No CTA | Gap | R14. One CTA, last 5 s (short) or 15 s (long) |
| Hook | Under-specified | Add word and second caps, visual hook in frame 1, promise match (R1 to R3) |

## 7. Patterns that repeat

1. The promise is written before the script (MrBeast, Abdaal, Veritasium "legit bait").
2. The first 3 s (short) or 30 s (long) only confirm the promise. No context, no credentials, no logo.
3. Show, then explain. Visual carries the exposition; voice adds what the picture cannot.
4. One question per video, paid off in steps. Long videos use sub-payoffs every 1 to 2 min and re-engagements at fixed marks.
5. Escalation: the best visual is saved for the payoff.
6. Simplicity is policed, by drafts (Kurzgesagt), rules (MrBeast) or outline-first (Very Normal).
7. Endings are short. Either abrupt after the payoff or a fixed ritual line.
8. Facts are sourced. Every educator with a durable audience shows sources or uses experts. The one documented 2026 failure was unverified AI notes.

## 8. What does NOT transfer to a small outdoor tour operator

| Practice | Why not | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| MrBeast spectacle re-engagements ("only MrBeast can do this") | Needs money, stunts, crew | Re-engage with a place reveal: the view at the top, the first powder turn, the waffle at the café |
| Kurzgesagt full animation, 1,200 hours | Budget and time | Real footage plus simple map or number cards |
| 10 to 20 min explainer arcs | Our hard cap is 200 s | Compress: 3 to 4 segments, 40 to 60 s each |
| Nas Daily "shouty" delivery | Breaks the warm, calm brand voice | 2.4 to 2.8 wps calm read; energy from footage |
| Fast cut rate as a goal | Outdoor footage needs time to read; Joos says novelty matters more than cuts | New shot each sentence, longer holds on landscapes in long form |
| Misconception framed as "you are wrong" | Client bans negative framing | Frame as discovery: "Most people think ski touring is for experts. Day one starts on gentle slopes." |
| Clickbait claims, superlatives | Client fact rules and Norwegian marketing law (see 02, section 7) | Accurate promise; no "best", "only", no guaranteed snow or aurora |
| Abrupt ending with no CTA | A tour operator needs a next step | Payoff, then one concrete CTA line, then cut |
| Daily output (Nas Daily) | Team size | Batch from one shoot day, using the shot-list outline |
