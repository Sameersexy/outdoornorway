# Psychology for scripts, with honest grades

Use the higher-graded lever when two compete. Never write "studies show" without a source you can name.

Grades: A peer reviewed or platform official. B large dataset or documented process. C practitioner lore. Not supported: tested and failed, or no source.

## What keeps people watching

| Lever | What it means in a script | Grade | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Open one specific question the video answers. Curiosity improves memory for the answer and for what is shown while the viewer is curious | A | Gruber, Gelman, Ranganath 2014, Neuron; Kang et al 2009 |
| Resumption tendency | People tend to go back to an interrupted task. The honest basis for an open loop | A | Ghibellini and Meier 2025 meta-analysis (Ovsiankina effect) |
| Misconception first | Voice the common belief, then show the result. Clear explanation alone was rated easy and taught little | A | Muller, PhD research, reported in THE Journal 2015 |
| Segmenting and signalling | Chapters and progress lines help viewers follow | A | Mayer, Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning |
| Redundancy | Narration plus identical on-screen text hurts. Short keyword overlays help | A | Mayer; Yue, Bjork and Bjork 2013 |
| Working memory | About 4 chunks at once. Hence 1 fact per short beat, 2 per long beat | A | Cowan 2001, 2010 |
| Thumbnail and title match | The first 30 s must confirm the promise | A | YouTube Help, audience retention |
| Escalation | Each step bigger than the last, best payoff late | B | MrBeast production memo |

## Interruption and emotional relevance

Kept from the previous skill, with the claims trimmed. A hook's job is to interrupt a thumb that is about to scroll. Polite, generic openers lose that contest; a specific, slightly uncomfortable truth wins it.

The best source of that truth is emotional relevance: what the viewer believes but has never heard said out loud. "You think you'll be the slow one." "Your kids don't ski, so you assume this trip isn't for you." Say it plainly, then let the video answer it with a fact. Find it in the objection list (references/brand-and-claims.md, product-marketing.md) or in real guest questions, never by guessing at the viewer's age, body or status (BR08).

Grade: C for the craft claim. The memory benefit of curiosity behind it is A (Gruber 2014).

## What we dropped

| Claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Zeigarnik: unfinished tasks are remembered better | Not supported | Ghibellini and Meier 2025: 59 studies, recall ratio 0.99, "no memory advantage for unfinished tasks" |
| Each cut or reveal is a "dopamine hit" | Not supported | Dopamine signals reward prediction and learning, not pleasure per cut. No source found |
| Hold the main answer until the very end, always | Fine for 25 s, risky for 200 s | Mayer favours clear segments (A). YouTube dips mark where viewers leave (A). Use mini-payoffs |
| Fast cutting keeps people watching | Weak | Mario Joos: "It's not the cutting speed, it's the speed at which you introduce progressive novel information" (C) |

## What moves a guest toward booking

| Lever | Script pattern | Grade | Misuse to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specificity | Nouns, times, sounds: "the squeak of dry snow under your skins" | B (Heath, Made to Stick) | False precision, invented times |
| Identity by behaviour | "You check the forecast on Wednesday and plan the weekend by Thursday" | C | Identity by age, health or status (BR08, Meta personal attributes policy) |
| Social proof | One real guest detail with name, month and trip, or a rating with platform, count and date | A (Google 2020: largest or second-largest lever in 28 of 31 categories) | Invented quotes, a rating without a source. Needs a facts row |
| Authority | A named guide doing a visible expert thing: reading the forecast, digging a pit | B | Unverifiable credentials |
| Risk reversal | The real cancellation line, word for word | B | "Risk-free" with conditions |
| Experiences over things | The memory outlasts a purchase | A (Van Boven and Gilovich 2003) | Promising happiness |
| Anticipation | The weeks before the trip are part of it | A (Kumar, Killingsworth, Gilovich 2014) | Promising content you do not send |
| Scarcity | Only a real, dated, operational deadline | Low effect, legal risk | Countdowns, "few spots left" (BR04, Norwegian Marketing Control Act) |

## Anxieties for a ski or outdoor trip

Answer one anxiety per short, one per chapter in a long. Answer it with a fact about the trip, never with a question about the viewer.

| Anxiety | What the viewer is really asking | Answer with | Never write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Am I fit enough? | "Will I be the problem?" | The day profile: hours, ascent, stops, who sets the pace | "Out of shape?" |
| Am I good enough? | "Will I embarrass myself?" | The level in plain terms: "confident on red runs" | "Are you a beginner?" |
| Will I hold the group up? | "Will they wait for me?" | "The guide sets the pace" (only if true) | Shaming a slow skier |
| Will there be snow? | "Is the money safe?" | Season window, what happens if conditions are poor | "Guaranteed snow", "perfect powder" (BR09) |
| Is it safe? | Avalanche, injury | How the route is chosen, from the facts table | Accident imagery, "survive" (BR05) |
| Will I see the aurora? | "Is the trip worth it if not?" | The trip stands without it; the sky is a possibility | Any promise (BR09, BR10) |
| Is it worth the money? | "What do I get?" | The total price, what is and is not included | "Best value" |
| Will I be cold? | "Will I be miserable?" | Layers, warm stops, the hut | "Never cold" |
| How do I get there? | "Is it complicated?" | Train from Bergen, about an hour | Invented transfer times |
| Will I fit in? | "Who else goes?" | Group described by behaviour | Age or status descriptions |

## The loop test, kept from the previous skill

After each line ask: which loop does this open, sustain or close? If none, cut it. This is the most useful idea in the old script-writing skill, and it survives with better reasons.
