# Long form playbook: 200 seconds

For YouTube landscape (16:9). Hard max 200 s (3:20), warn above 190 s. 440 words hard max, 320 to 400 target, 14 to 22 beats. Limits in tiers.md.

## Styles in long form

Two styles work here. Set `style:` in the front matter; without it the script is `voiceover`. `ugc_text` is short only and is a parse error in a long script.

| | voiceover | ugc_talk |
|---|---|---|
| Who | A calm read over edited footage | A guide talking to the camera and narrating live on the day |
| Words per second | 2.2 | 2.8 |
| Words, hard max (target) | 440 (320 to 400) | 560 (420 to 520) |
| Sentence hard max | 18 | 20 |
| Everything else | The long tier | The long tier: hook by 8 s, promise by 30 s, 4 to 6 chapters, the same fact and number limits |

ugc_talk long adds the UGC rules: a person, selfie, POV, handheld shot or hands in frame one (U01), no end card, logo, title card or stock footage anywhere (U02), a hook that says you or opens on a first-person stake (U03), a soft call to action (U05) and a named subject in every visual (U07). Disclosure (D01) and music (SND01) apply in both styles. The long format that worked for creators is one day, start to finish: hook in 10 s, 4 to 6 chapters of 25 to 40 s, close on the group, landscape, the guide narrating live, not in post (references/ugc-style.md). In ugc_talk, the End row in the B-roll table below drops the end card: close on the group at the van.

A 200 s video is a chain of small units around one question. A single question held open for three minutes with nothing paid off is a plateau, and plateaus are where the retention curve dips.

## Shape

| Time | Part | Roles | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-8 | Hook | hook, open:A | 22 max (17 fits 8 s) | One question, motion on screen, "you" in the line |
| 8-30 | Promise | promise | 70 max up to its end | What the viewer will know by the end. Confirms the title |
| 30-60 | Chapter 1 | ch:1, mini:1 | 60 to 80 | Small question, small answer |
| 55-80 | Re-engage | reengage, progress | inside chapter 2 | A new view, a new question |
| 60-100 | Chapter 2 | ch:2, mini:2 | 60 to 80 | |
| 80-120 | Partial answer | partial | inside chapter 3 | Half of the main answer, honestly |
| 100-130 | Chapter 3 | ch:3, mini:3 | 60 to 80 | |
| 120-145 | Re-engage | reengage, progress | inside chapter 4 | The big reveal shot |
| 130-165 | Chapter 4 (and 5, 6 if needed) | ch:4, mini:4 | 60 to 80 | |
| by 185 | Full answer | payoff, close:A | 25 to 30 | Close the dominant loop |
| last 20 s | Call to action | cta | 25 max | One step, tied to the payoff |

4 chapters is the default. Use 5 or 6 only when each still gets 2 beats and a mini-payoff.

The table is the frame every long script must fit. Inside it, pick one of three structures so scripts do not share one skeleton.

| Structure | Chapters are | Dominant question | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| A day as scenes | Moments in time order: the start, the climb, the top, the way down | "Could you do this day?" | One product, one day |
| Misconception test | Beliefs the viewer holds, each tested on camera | "Is [belief] true?" | An objection that stops bookings |
| Three people, one trip | One guest per chapter, each with one want; the last chapter brings them together | "Can one trip suit all of them?" | Mixed groups, families, a non-skier in the party |

Rules for all three: chapters are 2 to 4 beats, each chapter opens and closes its own small question, the strongest shot goes late.

If the facts table holds fewer than about 8 rows, lower `seconds` (150 s is a good long script) or accept the W02 warning. Never pad the word budget with claims the table does not hold.

## Hook and promise

- Verbal hook by 8 s, at most 22 words. 17 is what fits 8 s at 2.2 words per second.
- No channel intro, no greeting, no logo sting. Show, then speak.
- The promise beat ends by 30 s. YouTube measures "Intro" as the share still watching after 30 s, and a weak intro means the first 30 s did not match the title (A).
- Say what the viewer will be able to decide or do, in this video's words. Do not reuse "By the end, you'll know if it's for you" or any example promise.
- Write the title before the script. The promise repeats its key noun.

## Chapters and mini-payoffs

- Each chapter opens a small question (open:B, close:B) and answers it inside the chapter (mini:N).
- At most 2 loops open at once: the dominant one and the current chapter's.
- Order chapters so stakes or views rise. Save the strongest shot for the last chapter or the payoff (MrBeast "stair stepping", B).
- Name the chapter in an overlay on its opening beat ("The kit", "The pace"). It doubles as a YouTube chapter title (needs 3 or more chapters of at least 10 s each, B).

## Progress markers

A progress line at each chapter seam tells the viewer where they are. It is a signalling cue (Mayer, A) placed at the likely dip points. Vary the shape; the lines below show shapes, not text to copy.

- Done and next: "Skins on, heels free. The next part is the pace."
- Position in space: "Half the height is below you now."
- Position in time: "It's ten o'clock, and the trees are thinning."
- The viewer's own question: "Which leaves the one thing people ask on the drive up."
- A visual seam with no words: a 2 s sound break and a chapter overlay.

Avoid end signals. "That's it", "to wrap up", "in conclusion" are blocked (BR02).

## Partial answer

Between 80 and 120 s, give half of the main answer in plain sentences, then name what is still open. No colon reveal ("Here's half the answer: ..."). Shapes:

- "Your legs can do this part. Finding the way up is the harder half."
- "Cold hands are normal for ten minutes. Wet layers are what the guide watches."
- "The kids will be fine on snowshoes. The open question is the adults."
- "Most of the climb is walking. The last hour is different."

## Re-engagement beats

Between 55 and 80 s and again between 120 and 145 s. A re-engagement is a change the viewer can see or hear:

- A place reveal: the view above the tree line, the fjord from the top.
- A new person: the guide on camera answering one question, or a guest saying one honest line.
- A sound break: skins squeaking, wind, a thermos lid, 2 to 3 s marked `[PAUSE 2]`.
- A new question that the next chapter answers.
- A small mishap: a dropped glove sliding downhill, a laugh, the guide's dry comment.

Spectacle budgets do not transfer. A view and a question do.

## Visuals and B-roll vocabulary

- Shot change every 3 to 6 s. Landscape holds up to 6 s are fine when the voice is quiet.
- Every beat over 6 s lists 2 or more shots. A 40 s stretch with only single-shot beats and no shift or reengage gets a warning (V03).
- Write the visual column as a shot list the crew can film on one day.

| Category | Shots |
|---|---|
| Kit | skins unrolled, binding flipped to walk mode, boots clicked in, pack zipped |
| Climb | skin track from above, line of skiers, breath steaming, poles planted, a guide glancing back |
| Guide | reading the forecast, digging a small pit, pointing at a line, a short briefing |
| Place | the ridge at dawn, birch in snow, a frozen lake, the fjord from the top, the gondola |
| People | a thermos passed hand to hand, a laugh at a rest stop, a waffle at a mountain cafe |
| Descent | skins off, the guide's pitch, slow turns, tracks from below |
| End | the group back at the van, the end card with the link |

## Call to action

- Exactly one cta beat, starting in the last 20 s of the declared end, max 25 words (C01, C02, C03).
- Optional one soft-cta beat mid-video. It must start after 30 s and after the first mini-payoff (C02). Keep it under 10 words (guidance, not checked). That makes 2 asks at most.
- The CTA is content: name the next concrete step tied to the payoff, and the destination ("the link below"). That page must sell the same tour.
- One ask verb per beat. Two or more in the cta beat warn (C03). A booking or link ask in a story beat warns (C01).
- A long beat holds 1 number. "12 or 19 Feb 2027" is 3, so full dates go in the description, backed by a verified facts row.
- No "like and subscribe" (BR02).

## Weak versus strong

| Weak | Why it fails | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| "Hi everyone, welcome back to the channel!" | Greeting, BR01 | "You ride chairlifts all winter. Could you climb this mountain by lunch?" |
| A 60 s intro on the company's history | Promise arrives after 30 s, company is the subject | Promise beat by 19 s that names what the viewer can decide by the end |
| One question, answered only in the last 10 s | Plateau, no mini-payoffs | Each chapter answers its own question |
| "Day 1 Bergen, day 2 Voss, day 3 Myrkdalen..." | Itinerary reading, fact stacking | One day told as scenes: the climb, the top, the way down |
| "We guarantee the best snow in Norway." | BR09 and BR11 | "The guide picks the slope from this week's snow." |
| "That's all for today, thanks for watching!" | End signal, BR02 | Payoff line, then the CTA, then cut |

## Checklist before running the validator

- [ ] Style set. ugc_talk long: no end card or logo in any visual, disclosure beat starting by 5 s when relationship needs one.
- [ ] The title promise is spoken by 30 s.
- [ ] 4 to 6 chapters, each with a mini-payoff.
- [ ] Partial answer between 80 and 120 s.
- [ ] Re-engagement beats in both windows.
- [ ] Progress line on each chapter seam.
- [ ] Dominant loop closed by 185 s and before the CTA.
- [ ] At most 14 facts, 6 numbers, 1 number per beat. A date with a day and a year is 2 numbers.
- [ ] One honest downside or limit, stated plainly.
- [ ] No progress, partial or promise line copied from the examples or this page.
