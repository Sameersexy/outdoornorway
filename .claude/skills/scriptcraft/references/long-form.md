# Long form playbook: 200 seconds

For YouTube landscape (16:9). Hard max 200 s (3:20), warn above 190 s. 440 words hard max, 320 to 400 target, 14 to 22 beats. Limits in tiers.md.

A 200 s video is a chain of small units around one question. A single question held open for three minutes with nothing paid off is a plateau, and plateaus are where the retention curve dips.

## Shape

| Time | Part | Roles | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-8 | Hook | hook, open:A | 22 max (17 fits 8 s) | One question, motion on screen, "you" in the line |
| 8-30 | Promise | promise | 70 max up to its end | What the viewer will know by the end. Confirms the title |
| 30-60 | Chapter 1 | ch:1, mini:1 | 60 to 80 | Small question, small answer |
| 55-80 | Re-engage | reengage, progress | inside chapter 2 | A new view, a new question |
| 60-100 | Chapter 2 | ch:2, mini:2 | 60 to 80 | |
| 80-120 | Partial answer | partial | inside chapter 3 | Half of the main answer, honestly |
| 100-130 | Chapter 3 | ch:3, mini:3 | 60 to 80 | |
| 120-145 | Re-engage | reengage, progress | inside chapter 4 | The big reveal shot |
| 130-165 | Chapter 4 (and 5, 6 if needed) | ch:4, mini:4 | 60 to 80 | |
| by 185 | Full answer | payoff, close:A | 25 to 30 | Close the dominant loop |
| last 20 s | Call to action | cta | 25 max | One step, tied to the payoff |

4 chapters is the default. Use 5 or 6 only when each still gets 2 beats and a mini-payoff.

## Hook and promise

- Verbal hook by 8 s, at most 22 words. 17 is what fits 8 s at 2.2 words per second.
- No channel intro, no greeting, no logo sting. Show, then speak.
- The promise beat ends by 30 s. YouTube measures "Intro" as the share still watching after 30 s, and a weak intro means the first 30 s did not match the title (A).
- Write the title before the script. The promise repeats its key noun.

## Chapters and mini-payoffs

- Each chapter opens a small question (open:B, close:B) and answers it inside the chapter (mini:N).
- At most 2 loops open at once: the dominant one and the current chapter's.
- Order chapters so stakes or views rise. Save the strongest shot for the last chapter or the payoff (MrBeast "stair stepping", B).
- Name the chapter in an overlay on its opening beat ("The kit", "The pace"). It doubles as a YouTube chapter title (needs 3 or more chapters of at least 10 s each, B).

## Progress markers

A progress line at each chapter seam tells the viewer where they are. It is a signalling cue (Mayer, A) placed at the likely dip points.

- "That's the kit, sorted. Now the question people ask us."
- "You're halfway up the mountain."
- Avoid end signals. "That's it", "to wrap up", "in conclusion" are blocked (BR02).

## Partial answer

Between 80 and 120 s, give half of the main answer: "Here's half the answer: your legs can do this. The harder part is knowing where to go." It rewards the viewer who stayed and opens the next chapter.

## Re-engagement beats

Between 55 and 80 s and again between 120 and 145 s. A re-engagement is a change the viewer can see or hear:

- A place reveal: the view above the tree line, the fjord from the top.
- A new person: the guide on camera answering one question.
- A sound break: skins squeaking, wind, a thermos lid, 2 to 3 s without voice.
- A new question that the next chapter answers.

Spectacle budgets do not transfer. A view and a question do.

## Visuals and B-roll vocabulary

- Shot change every 3 to 6 s. Landscape holds up to 6 s are fine when the voice is quiet.
- Every beat over 6 s lists 2 or more shots. A 40 s stretch with only single-shot beats and no shift or reengage gets a warning (V03).
- Write the visual column as a shot list the crew can film on one day.

| Category | Shots |
|---|---|
| Kit | skins unrolled, binding flipped to walk mode, boots clicked in, pack zipped |
| Climb | skin track from above, line of skiers, breath steaming, poles planted, a guide glancing back |
| Guide | reading the forecast, digging a small pit, pointing at a line, a short briefing |
| Place | the ridge at dawn, birch in snow, a frozen lake, the fjord from the top, the gondola |
| People | a thermos passed hand to hand, a laugh at a rest stop, a waffle at a mountain cafe |
| Descent | skins off, the guide's pitch, slow turns, tracks from below |
| End | the group back at the van, the end card with the link |

## Call to action

- One primary CTA, starting in the last 20 s, max 25 words.
- Optional one soft CTA mid-video (soft-cta), after the first mini-payoff. Keep it under 10 words (guidance, not checked). Never in the first 30 s.
- The CTA is content: name the next concrete step tied to the payoff. "Day tours run every Saturday and Sunday. Book a date from the link below."
- No stacked asks. No "like and subscribe" (BR02).

## Weak versus strong

| Weak | Why it fails | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| "Hi everyone, welcome back to the channel!" | Greeting, BR01 | "You ride chairlifts all winter. Could you climb this mountain by lunch?" |
| A 60 s intro on the company's history | Promise arrives after 30 s, company is the subject | Promise beat by 19 s: "By the end, you'll know if it's for you." |
| One question, answered only in the last 10 s | Plateau, no mini-payoffs | Each chapter answers its own question |
| "Day 1 Bergen, day 2 Voss, day 3 Myrkdalen..." | Itinerary reading, fact stacking | One day told as scenes: the climb, the top, the way down |
| "We guarantee the best snow in Norway." | BR09 and BR11 | "The guide picks the slope from this week's snow." |
| "That's all for today, thanks for watching!" | End signal, BR02 | Payoff line, then the CTA, then cut |

## Checklist before running the validator

- [ ] The title promise is spoken by 30 s.
- [ ] 4 to 6 chapters, each with a mini-payoff.
- [ ] Partial answer between 80 and 120 s.
- [ ] Re-engagement beats in both windows.
- [ ] Progress line on each chapter seam.
- [ ] Dominant loop closed by 185 s and before the CTA.
- [ ] At most 14 facts, 6 numbers, 1 number per beat.
- [ ] One honest downside or limit, stated plainly.
