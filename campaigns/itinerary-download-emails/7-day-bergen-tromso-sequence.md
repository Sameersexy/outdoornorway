## Email 4, day 9: what if we see nothing

Reader's question: what if the lights do not show?
Step we ask for: accept the real odds and keep going.

Subject A: What if the lights do not show?
Subject B: Three evenings, a guide who moves, and the night after 9 pm
Preview: Nobody can promise the aurora. Here is how the trip stacks the odds.

Hi [first name],

Nobody can promise you the aurora, and I would not trust anyone who does. The lights are a natural phenomenon, and no departure can guarantee an appearance. So here is how the trip improves your chances, and what you still have if the sky stays shut.

Three evenings. Day 4 is a guided aurora safari on the evening you land in Tromsø. Day 5 ends with aurora observation at the hotel camp. Day 6 is a night-time aurora hunt after the dog sledding. Our guide to the lights says it in four words: think in nights, not hours.

A guide who reads the sky. On a safari night your guide checks the forecasts, the cloud cover and the road conditions, and sometimes drives a long way to find a gap in the clouds. Tromsø sits at about 69 degrees north, directly beneath the aurora oval, which is why it is one of the world's most established places to see the lights.

Patience after 9 pm. Activity is often strongest between 20:00 and 02:00. Displays can appear suddenly, disappear and return later, so the night is not over because nothing has happened by 9 pm.

If the sky stays cloudy. You still have the fjord by private boat on Day 5, the husky teams on Day 6, the snowshoe and ski days in Voss, and Bergen's seven mountains on Day 1.
